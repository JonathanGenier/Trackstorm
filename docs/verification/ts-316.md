## Implemented

- Default deployed mass is **18000 kg**, six times the default car mass; validated
  tuning range is 50–60000 kg. Standing linear damping is 5. Existing captured mass
  and saved overrides remain valid. Ordinary contact moves the wall slightly;
  powerful impacts can displace and yaw it. Existing terrain-relative pitch/roll
  stabilization, geometry, HP, weapon interactions and destruction rules remain.
- Shield momentum is applied once by Core. The generic native prop impulse now
  excludes Shields. Simultaneous cars reconstruct their incoming velocity from the
  same pre-impact wall boundary, then account for earlier impulses in that batch.
  Deterministic tests check momentum conservation and absence of energy injection.
- Vehicle contacts with a deployed Shield use the established solid-obstacle
  incidence-weighted severity, damage thresholds/cap and victim cooldown. Contact
  manifolds do not introduce another damage path. Center, wings, glancing angles,
  duplicate contacts and resting pressure are covered.
- Standing Shield terrain fitting caches a successful unchanged static footprint;
  translation, yaw or a significant vertical move refits it. Unsuccessful fits are
  retried. Plane fitting stops early when its queried basis converges, while retaining
  all footprint probes, support/clearance requirements and the four-pass bound.
  The cache is native per-body state, reconstructed after recovery, not serialized.
- Added opt-in native profiling and heavy-contact scenarios; expanded terrain checks
  to two sixteen-wall deployment/soak/destruction cycles. Updated affected feature
  documentation and verification routing. No new assets, dependencies, protocol fields,
  Shared layer, or non-Shield vehicle-handling changes were introduced.

## Measured FPS regression and cause

Windows 10, pinned Godot **4.7.2 .NET**, OpenGL Compatibility, NVIDIA GTX 1070,
driver 582.66. The rendered fixture runs two independent 1280×720 arena views and
caps rendering at 60 FPS. FPS uses Stopwatch elapsed time across 180 rendered frames;
terrain work is timed directly inside `ShieldGround`, with fit/ray counters.
Godot process/physics, active-body, node and orphan monitors are also logged. Their
sampling periods differ; those monitor durations are not summed into frame time.

The baseline ran the original production behavior plus measurement instrumentation.
The isolated after-run changed terrain fitting only, before the mass/damping change.
Both completed the same sixteen-wall, 1800-physics-frame terrain soak.

| Run / sample | FPS | Terrain-fit ms per render frame | Fits / rays per 180 render frames |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Original, six walls | 32.9 | 13.378 | 1601 / 256160 |
| Original, sixteen walls, first window | 6.3 | 105.527 | 13201 / 2112160 |
| Original, sixteen walls, settled window | **4.1** | **179.605** | **23040 / 3686400** |
| Terrain optimization only, sixteen settled walls | **58.3–60.2**, mean 59.7 over nine windows | 0.000 | 0 / 0 |
| Final heavy defaults, synthetic terrain, two cycles | 54.3–60.2, mean 58.8 over nineteen settled windows | 0.000 | 0 / 0 |
| Final heavy defaults, actual production map, two cycles | 44.6–51.8, mean 50.4 over sixteen settled windows | 0.000 | 0 / 0 |

**Identified cause:** every stationary wall repeated its complete 40-probe footprint
fit for four passes on every physics step: 160 native ray intersections per wall per
step. At sixteen walls the measured 23040 fits per 180 render frames correspond to
eight physics catch-up steps per rendered frame. This repeated query work consumed
179.605 ms per rendered frame in the worst recorded steady window. The controlled
terrain-only change removed that work and restored the capped frame rate.

The original and isolated-after settled scenes both had **4431 nodes, zero orphans,
and 19 active bodies** (sixteen walls plus three fixture props). That comparison
does not support a lingering-object explanation for the reproduced collapse.
Final synthetic cleanup returned to 2127 nodes, three non-Shield bodies, and zero
orphans after both cycles. Production-map peak nodes were 8460/8459 across the cycles;
cleanup removed every Shield body and destruction effect on both peers, with zero
orphans and zero active bodies. The last production cleanup sample was 54.7 FPS.

The final production-map result renders substantially more scene content than the
synthetic comparison; it is not a single-player FPS promise. All recorded settled
windows, including slower transition windows, are included in the ranges above.
These runs establish removal of the reproduced cumulative fitting regression, not
an exhaustive allocation audit or performance guarantee on every machine.

## Native behavior observed

The final heavy run used two independent production arenas over real local UDP,
30 ms outbound delay, 5 ms jitter and 2% packet loss. It grants through existing
authority and deploys via ordinary selected-use input. The scenes seed poses and
impact velocities to isolate repeatable contacts; this is automated native runtime
evidence, not a human driving session.

| Scenario | Peak wall displacement | Vehicle HP lost | Wall HP remaining | Wall speed after five seconds |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Casual 3 m/s center contact | 0.085 m | 0 | 1000 | 0 |
| 18 m/s center setup | 0.489 m | 62.7 | 937.3 | 0 |
| 40 m/s center setup | 1.141 m | 100.0 | 900.0 | 0 |
| Left wing | 0.485 m | 63.1 | 936.9 | 0 |

All heavy scenarios stayed upright, avoided upward launches, obeyed damage cooldowns,
and matched accepted host Shield state and vehicle HP on the peer. Opposing car
impulses largely canceled; one wall damage application was retained. The ordinary
regression measured car slowdown **16.55 → 2.70 m/s**, approximately 0.48 m off-center
wall displacement with 15.6 degrees yaw, only 0.12 m support-relative car rise and
1.07 m/s upward speed. An unpowered wall starting at 8 m/s traveled 1.43 m and stopped
within three seconds.

Terrain coverage included flat, 20-degree slope, 30/35-degree banks, compound slope,
wing-local rise, partial-edge and uneven footing; nine size/heading combinations;
inverted-car placement; and missing/too-steep/excessively unsupported rejection.
Each sixteen-wall final soak ran 1800 physics frames, twice per map mode. Measured
settled drift and speed were both zero at the logged precision. Cache reuse did not
relax complete panel clearance or deployment obstruction checks.

The ordinary regression retained damage on deployment, blocked-use retry, forward/
reverse/banked release, late third-peer admission, Machine Gun destruction,
Missile damage **812.3 → 561.1 HP**, Salvo damage **561.1 → 61.1 HP**, stress deployment,
native side-ground breakage and exact expiry despite retuning. All peer colliders
were removed. Migration restored two retained pools with exact HP, pose, expiry,
tipping and damage watermark, without refilling or restarting timers.

### Corrections made during verification

- Standing damping 2 allowed 5.809 m travel during five seconds of engine pressure;
  damping 5 reduced that to 2.573 m while preserving physical movement and no damage ticks.
- The launch assertion now measures upward velocity rather than rejecting ordinary
  downward falls. Simultaneous opposing impacts correctly expect near-zero net movement.
- Production-map repeated deployment now uses opposite facing at the repeated seventh
  infield marker, avoiding intentional overlap rejection. Terrain setup vehicles receive
  extra fixture HP so teleports cannot kill them. Production collision/damage scenarios
  retain ordinary HP. Placement rules were not relaxed.
- The old tipping fixture assumed a 250 kg wall and also received the removed duplicate
  native prop impulse. The heavy default appropriately did not tip. Its legacy path now
  explicitly uses 250 kg and genuine 60/80 m/s high-energy setup speeds instead of 40/60.
  All five center/offset/fast/reverse/banked cases then visibly tipped and broke on native
  ground contact, with measured car rise/upward speed zero. Production tipping thresholds,
  falling friction/damping, HP and destruction logic were not changed to force a pass.

## Runtime review evidence and limitations

After the comprehensive gate and final heavy run, inspected rendered captures for
40 m/s center contact, wing contact, opposing cars and sustained pressure
(`heavy-2/4/6/7.png`). Also inspected the 35-degree terrain footing, banked legacy
fall, and production infield deployment captures. Captures are under
`.godot/world-wall-checks` and `.godot/world-wall-production-checks`; native numerical
traces provide motion/settling evidence that still images alone cannot establish.

- **VERIFIED:** scripted native collisions, repeated deployments, full-footprint terrain
  stability, measured FPS/ray-query improvement, lifecycle cleanup, live impaired UDP
  state agreement, late join and native authority migration.
- **INFERRED:** the measured reduction in casual movement and sharp car slowdown should
  feel substantially heavier in normal play. Final human handling/balance approval is
  not inferred from those measurements.
- **UNVERIFIED:** human controller/keyboard feel and fun, independent-machine or authenticated
  EOS/WAN behavior, extreme latency, multi-hour sessions, every custom mass/dimension,
  sixteen continuously moving walls, exhaustive allocation/GC profiling, and successful
  long-disconnect reconnect. Dedicated extended following-contact/environment-network
  suites were not run. Audio, camera polish and new VFX were not scored.
- Static-footprint caching assumes the eligible authored support does not move/change
  under an otherwise unchanged wall; dynamic/destructible terrain is not supported by
  this cache. No such terrain change was introduced here.
- Saved Shield mass overrides are preserved. For human testing, reset the Shield category
  or set mass to **18000**, enable Droppable pickup selection, give Shield positive weight
  and set competing item weights to zero using existing controls. Obtain/deploy repeatedly
  through normal pickups; no new spawning flow is required.
