## Implemented

- The existing authoritative spawn claim remains the single collectible. All
  three intact crates and their aura hide together; one new grant token emits one
  complete-stack smash. Core eligibility, the swept three-metre claim sphere,
  award arbitration, inventory, selection/RNG, spawn layout, cooldown and protocol
  remain unchanged. Weapon/projectile hits have no crate collision target.
- Match-owned token/revision projection suppresses duplicates, stale publications
  and historical admission/recovery effects. Recovery clears transient debris.
  A fresh host can render a claim in its first simulation publication; a joining
  client silently installs its historical baseline. Retained spawn tokens work
  independently of the claimant's current inventory or connection.
- One burst uses **72 wood fragments, 192 folded gold foil pieces, 30 gold stars,
  240 spark trails and a brief gold flash**. Six MultiMesh batches animate in
  shaders, with shared arena meshes/textures and no per-piece physics, networking
  or CPU frame updates. The authoritative grant token seeds the initial buffers.
- Pieces scatter immediately and progressively fade. The owner expires at
  **12 seconds**, including during gameplay pause. Newer confirmed ticks age
  delayed publications; already expired outcomes are skipped. Pressure above
  27 concurrent bursts retires older bursts over 0.2 seconds, with a hard limit
  of 54. Match-owner teardown frees active effects.
- Editable Blender splinter/foil/star masters, runtime GLB, shaders and hashed
  provenance accompany the feature. The intact asset, its textures, size, facing,
  fitted grounded poses and floating-jump exception remain intact. New wood uses
  the existing crimson/ivory/endgrain maps; the flash reuses the already licensed
  Kenney spark texture. No new external dependency was introduced.
- Targeted routing, projection tests, native smash checks and additional
  multiplayer/reconnect/infield assertions are included. Feature and asset
  documentation describe the current implementation and its limits.

## Assumptions, risks and explicitly unverified areas

- Jira described attached concept images but the inspected issues had no
  attachments. The local `Carnage Circus/Pickup/3 crates explosion.png` is the
  previously supplied TS-309/TS-310 explosion reference documented in TS-310's
  evidence. It was inspected alongside the intact reference and retained
  unchanged as `assets/items/carnival-crate/source/ApprovedSmash.png`. Mapping
  that file to Jira's `golden_confetti_circus_crate_explosion.png` is an explicit
  reference-identity assumption; its SHA-256 is in `smash-sources.json`.
- The three-metre swept vehicle-centre interaction remains intentional. The
  18 per-crate crossings are controlled authoritative observations, complemented
  by actual native cars/jump routes; they are not 18 physical mesh contact tests.
- Identical token-seeded buffers were checked across local peers. Arrival latency
  can shift effect start time; effects are not frame-locked across devices.
  Cosmetic debris uses a fitted floor plane rather than world collision.
- Under extreme same-frame overload the 54-burst hard ceiling can truncate an
  old fade. Ordinary expiry and the 0.2-second pressure fade were exercised.
- **UNVERIFIED:** authenticated remote EOS/P2P, NAT traversal, multiple physical
  machines, exported builds, other GPUs/renderers, uncapped GPU cost, multi-hour
  memory/performance soak and manual controller gameplay/subjective audio feel.
  Local native UDP impairment/recovery is evidence for its tested conditions only.
- No functional failure remains in the exercised scenarios. The reference-fidelity
  limitation below remains a human quality decision. CI has not run for this
  unpushed implementation.
