## Scope and baseline

Jira classifies it as a Bug under TS-229; the explicit human assignment establishes the delivery unit and `ts-275-pg` branch. The branch started clean from main `447eebe042d89dcf0e8d34aef34def779040f27a` (TS-274 merge PR 151). TS-267 merge `6e3e1b17` is also in that ancestry. Final synchronization fetched the same main. Story version is `0.2.21`, with Windows export versions `0.2.21.0`.

## Implemented and investigated

- Hosted/predicted vehicles now sweep an identity-transform native query body sharing their fixed chassis and optional rear-shield shapes. `From` supplies the requested pose. This avoids expanding the query AABB by transforming an already axis-aligned world bound back into local space and then into the requested pose. Native contact solving, recovery margin, collision masks and the four-slide limit remain in use. The query excludes its owner, mirrors shield enablement, has no collision layer, has a zero mask outside synchronous queries, and releases its RID on tree exit.
- Remaining displacement now respects the actual obstacle plane as well as the anti-climb response plane. The latter still controls momentum. A downward fall into an inclined, widening bank previously spent all four passes repeating a geometrically blocked downward sweep. Clipping displacement lets the vehicle slide down the face without changing Core's impulse/damage rules.
- Exposed-face ray result dictionaries are disposed immediately after use.
- Added an actual-map repeated fall/scrape harness, a frozen corpus of ten expensive tilted native queries, native lifecycle/mask/shield checks, fast-check routing, and rendered playtest timing output. Updated vehicle and vehicle-networking feature documents.
- Aligned the existing bank-UDP fixture's teardown with the rock/environment fixtures: queue arena destruction and drain 20 frames before exiting. This corrects fixture shutdown diagnostics without changing production audio or gameplay.

No terrain/render meshes, collision bake, collision importer, rock hulls, authored routes, jump tuning, or Core gameplay rules changed. TS-274's 40,211-triangle terrain bake and TS-267's rock hulls are retained.

### Root-cause evidence

Native bound expansion is visible in [Godot Physics's body-motion implementation](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/master/modules/godot_physics_3d/godot_space_3d.cpp). That inspected upstream source explains the mechanism; measurements below independently exercise installed `4.7.2.stable.mono.official.ed1daf0bf` and do not assume upstream master is its exact source revision.

Temporary per-sweep instrumentation on the west-bank 25 m/s, 1.2-radian case measured frames 4–54 as follows. The instrumentation was removed from production code. These earlier runs occurred under competing machine load, so absolute times are diagnostic rather than a clean final benchmark.

| Configuration | Mean observation time | Mean native sweeps per tick |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| TS-274 motion path | 31.513 ms | 4.000 |
| Identity query bounds only | 9.429 ms | 4.000 |
| Identity bounds plus geometric displacement clipping | 5.051 ms | 1.765 |

The identity-only trial had the same final position as the original case; geometric clipping deliberately changes the slide trajectory. [Ablation summary](ts-275-evidence/investigation-ablation.json). Collision density around the bank amplifies cost, but further geometry simplification was not selected: planar/density experiments either exceeded surface-fit constraints or provided less benefit than correcting bounds and repeated sweeps. All candidates remained ignored experiments. No local seam, overlap, or terrain/render mismatch was demonstrated as a separate cause. Repeated correction was observed in exhausted slide passes; contact-manifold churn was not independently established.

After the user left the machine idle, alternating native batches on ten frozen poses measured aggregate median costs of **60.945 ms original versus 16.713 ms corrected**, ratio **0.274** (72.6% lower). Each pose uses five batches of eight calls per method, alternating order. Hit/miss, travel and remainder equivalence passed within 5 mm. This compares methods in one process on identical geometry and poses. [Per-pose results](ts-275-evidence/query-comparison.json).

### Repeated fall/scrape timing

The matrix covers both banks, 3/12/25 m/s initial speeds, heading offsets 0/0.45/1.2 radians, two repetitions and both hosted and practice adapters: 72 cases × 420 ticks. Initial pitch is 0.25 radians and roll ±0.45 radians. Each case begins airborne beside the bridge at `(±7, 5.7, 14)`, presses toward the inner face while falling, then uses ordinary steering/throttle and reverse. All cases registered falling contacts and bounded movement; maximum step was 0.4565 m. Initial speed is not a claim about exact contact speed.

Across 36 hosted cases, largest per-case mean was **2.873 ms**, largest p95 **5.311 ms**, and largest p99 **8.484 ms**. There were 8,881 hosted contact frames. Maximum elapsed callback time was **29.295 ms**; 31 contact frames exceeded 16.667 ms and each contained a recorded managed-GC pause. Subtracting recorded GC duration leaves maximum 11.040 ms and no over-budget samples, but raw pauses remain part of elapsed time and are not discarded from pass statistics. The harness passes on raw per-case mean <8 ms and p95 <20 ms. [Metrics](ts-275-evidence/final-metrics.json).

These accelerated fixed-step native checks are not whole-client rendering measurements. Practice callback timings exclude the native rigid-body solve outside the callback. Rendered playtesting supplies separate frame-pacing evidence.

The separate-process network run measured client frame-time p95 17.530 ms, p99 24.275 ms and maximum 52.276 ms; simulation-step p95 3.972 ms. This is a clean network harness verdict, not a claim of perfectly uniform whole-client frame pacing. The impaired rock/bank fixtures use separate native physics worlds within one process; they complement rather than replace the separate-process vehicle run.

### Active rendered approach/fall playtest

Ran `handling_playtest.tscn -- --tunnel-scrape-playtest --handling-network` at 1152×648 with the real Compatibility renderer. Issued bounded throttle/steering/brake commands through the existing input protocol and inspected rendered captures and traces. The fixture uses a plain following camera with no shake. [Executed command script](ts-275-evidence/idle-playtest-commands.ps1), [segment outcomes](ts-275-evidence/idle-playtest.log), [timing metrics](ts-275-evidence/playtest-metrics.json), [runtime log](ts-275-evidence/render-runtime.log).

Six dirt-side approaches at initial 12/25 m/s, from both sides with 0/±0.15-radian heading offsets, each used 55 approach ticks, 100 fall ticks, 180 pressure/steer ticks and 180 reverse ticks: 3,090 physics ticks total.

- Both straight 25 m/s approaches launched beside the bridge and fell into the opposite inner bank. The fall phases registered 35/31 contact frames, including 21/20 contacts preceded by downward velocity. Cars settled at approximately `(-6.582, 1.147, 14.685)` and `(6.586, 1.182, 14.543)` with ordinary impact damage. [Airborne approach](ts-275-evidence/approach-1-25-0.png), [bottom of fall](ts-275-evidence/approach-1-25-0-fall.png).
- Sustained pressure registered 148/180 contact frames. The west approach's pressure interval retained HP 895.731 and stable bank-side placement; reverse cleared the original face and finished at `(-3.802, 1.143, 29.220)`. The east reverse crossed the tunnel and contacted the opposite face, so it is recovery from the original contact region, not a contact-free end state. [Pressure](ts-275-evidence/approach--1-25-0-press.png), [reverse recovery](ts-275-evidence/approach--1-25-0-reverse.png).
- The 12 m/s east approach with a 0.15-radian offset reached a falling bank contact (16 contact frames, two preceded by downward velocity), then sustained pressure and reverse. [Capture](ts-275-evidence/approach-1-12-0.15-fall.png). Its west counterpart mostly missed the face and continued through a route/jump; it is a coverage attempt, not a repeated-bank-contact success.
- The ±0.15-radian 25 m/s approaches contacted the upper bank/bridge region and remained there under pressure, then reversed clear. They provide adjacent-contact coverage rather than proof of the lower falling-bank repro.

Across the 12 fall/pressure segments, largest observation mean was 3.689 ms, largest p95 4.102 ms, and maximum 14.293 ms. Largest sampled render p95 was 9.461 ms and maximum 25.856 ms. There was no repeated long collision-processing stall in these traces. Render intervals are measured between `_Process` callbacks and sampled by physics ticks; this is not a complete unique-render-frame capture or proof against display tearing.

The first approach recorded one **407.159 ms** render interval at tick 17, X=23.323, with zero contacts and a 0.418 ms observation callback. It occurred before the bridge/bank contact. Startup/shader work is a plausible explanation, not an established diagnosis. It is retained in the raw metrics; no whole-session hitch-free claim is made. Runtime stderr was empty. This was active command/observe playtesting, not continuous human controller play or an audio listening session.

## Assumptions, limitations and explicitly unverified areas

- An earlier matrix under competing workload failed one absolute timing gate (`network-fall-25-1.20--1-1`: mean 17.159 ms, p95 46.763 ms). It is not presented as a pass. The same completed implementation passed the subsequent sequential idle-machine run. Some earlier pauses coincided with GC; not every scheduling pause was diagnosed.
- Initial builds encountered NuGet vulnerability-endpoint error NU1900 and used command-scoped `NuGetAudit=false` during iteration. Final unmodified `check.ps1` restored and passed normally without that override.
- The first bank-network run and a verbose diagnostic rerun passed gameplay assertions but failed shutdown diagnostics with 40/30 leaked objects and two retained music resources. The [verbose log](ts-275-evidence/world-collision-network-retest.log) identifies audio streams/playback instances, not collision bodies. The existing fixture quit immediately; using the rock/environment fixtures' queued teardown and 20-frame drain produced the clean final run above. These earlier failures are retained, not reclassified as passes. Production audio was not changed.
- The frozen corpus and map matrix establish representative behavior, not mathematical equivalence for every pose. Near-threshold contacts can differ with query bounds or geometric clipping; regression suites and shield/lifecycle checks are material evidence.
- The query assumes chassis/shield local transforms and shape resources are fixed after construction, matching the current vehicle. Enablement and owner mask changes are supported and tested.
- No universal frame-time guarantee, long-duration soak, exported build, remote physical machine, Internet/EOS collision session, or physical-controller ergonomics is claimed. Original human screenshots/video were not attached in Jira; the approach line was reconstructed from its description and the map. Human confirmation of that exact line remains unverified.
