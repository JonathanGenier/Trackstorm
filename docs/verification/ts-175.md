## Implementation

- Original Blender master, reproducible authoring script, rigid-joint animation
  study, game GLB, seven original grain/patina materials, provenance/hashes and
  a read-only geometry/rig audit under `assets/items/salvo`.
- Telescoping wooden spoon, hollow oval bowl with steel ribs/rivets and rolled
  rim, forward sliding full-yaw bearing, mechanical stop buffers, telescoping
  spring support, compressed coil and visible winch/drum inside the production rack.
- The coil and support plate are turret children. The fixed winch cable routes
  through the bearing axis to the spoon underside. No duplicate vehicle rack.
- One flat row of five horizontal pyrotechnics on the spoon handle, with captive
  escapement and bowl retainers. The loaded round moves along the handle into
  the bowl in vehicle-local coordinates; inverted poses do not drop ammunition.
- Existing rack/trunk ownership and outgoing-weapon handoff remain intact.
  Catapult extension starts only at full rack height. Return centers yaw, finishes
  release, nests the assembly and then permits rack lowering/trunk closure.
- Accepted selection/count/use drives deployment and the 0.48-second release,
  45-degree stop/recoil, winch-reset and reload presentation. Remaining rounds do
  not respawn during ordinary five-round consumption.
- Reuses the validated accepted aim channel for yaw. Salvo remains outside
  direct-fire sticky assistance and keeps its existing local ground guide.
  Accepted shot-token succession preserves turret heading while old-token input
  stays invalid. Recovery reconstructs current ownership without replaying shots.
- Adds the mechanical native harness to the relevant fast-check route and updates
  focused system/asset documentation. Version properties and export presets remain
  unchanged as explicitly requested.
- Preserves the existing forward ground guide, but refreshes terrain sampling early
  when its target moves a metre. Final native testing exposed over four metres of
  guide lag during steering under the old fixed 50 ms throttle. Stationary sampling
  remains throttled; no aiming range or projectile target rules changed.

## Integration boundaries and limitations

- Current Salvo gameplay still requires five separate presses and uses its existing
  forward ground target, launch position, arc and effects. The new turret does not
  change those into camera-directed catapult projectile flight. TS-211 owns the
  committed automatic volley and mechanical launch gating; TS-212–214 own their
  separate flight/effects/final integration requirements.
- `Bowl`, `IsReady`, `Cycling` and `ObserveRounds` are the mechanical integration
  seams. Fuse ignition/flight/lifetime behavior is not authored here.
- Physical magazine presentation is capped at five. Existing non-default DevTools
  inventories above five retain their prior gameplay count; this presentation does
  not change that tuning or the later five-shot controller's contract.
- Ordinary deployment/reconstruction animation remains locally timed, as in the
  existing rack. Both peers consume accepted state, but no new phase-clock packet
  guarantees identical intermediate animation frames after late admission.
- Native tests use local direct-IP UDP. Independent-PC/authenticated EOS,
  physical-controller ergonomics, human chase-camera game feel and sustained
  eight-player GPU profiling are not claimed.
- Extreme poses include fixture-authored airborne native simulation and explicit
  visual-root roll/inversion checks. These are not a claim that a human drove every
  jump, collision and recovery combination.
