## Implemented

- Original infield shader detail: gravel, erosion, fine cracks, soil/grass
  variation, wet-bank breakup and muddy sheen; water normal ripples and shoreline
  treatment. No authored mesh, surface field, collision or gameplay tuning changed.
- Wheel-specific TS-77 identity drives dark Asphalt/Concrete sliding marks,
  disturbed Dirt, exposed-soil-colored Grass, dark Mud and heavier Deep Mud tracks.
  Water uses pooled transient wakes and splash spray, never persistent tire marks.
- Per vehicle: 512 track segments, 32 wake rings and four 24-particle emitters.
  Expiry, overwrite, distance culling, density reduction, life/airborne continuity
  breaks and disposal bound runtime resources. No physical terrain deformation.
- Clear Blue, Night, Ember Sky, Apocalypse and Neon Sunset packages configure
  sky/clouds/stars/sun or moon, directional light, ambient, reflection, terrain fog
  and exposure/tonemapping. Static preset selection; no clock or automatic cycling.
- `environment.preset` joins the existing authoritative configuration catalog;
  named Configs dropdown, normal Apply/Cancel/Reset, persistence, reliable revision
  ordering and existing recovery. Configuration wire schema 11 has 101 values.
  Arena rendering updates without rebuilding map content.
- Core authority/validation/persistence/checkpoint tests, native surface/VFX and
  eight-car repeated-use harness, Configs/recovery scenarios and fast-check routes.
  Updated durable feature docs and original shader/particle provenance.

## Evidence limits

Surface fixture captures use a flat test pad whose material is changed between
scenarios. Old marks from earlier dry scenarios can remain elsewhere on that pad;
Water assertions compare mark counts before/after wet interaction. This fixture
is not a substitute for the production-map surface and collision checks.

Tire marks and particles are local cosmetic approximations and are not required
to be pixel-identical across peers; the selected environment identity is shared.
Remote cosmetic contact timing can differ with interpolation. Density is an
internal hook for future optimization settings, not a new settings UI.

Authenticated multi-device EOS, exported builds, physical-controller ergonomics,
long-duration soak beyond the recorded runs and GPU profiling across hardware
are not verified. No claim of physical deformation, displaced soil or changed
collision/handling is made.

Normal practice was launched for direct keyboard/desktop inspection, but Windows
Graphics Capture failed with `SetIsBorderRequired: No such interface supported`.
The temporary session was stopped. No successful direct manual keyboard playtest
is claimed. Driving, UI input and camera tests above are automated native runtime

**What Was Exercised — VERIFIED:** native input-driven land/water driving,
all surface responses, eight-car repeated emissions, normal Configs Apply on
both participants, persistence/reconnect/migration, all five rendered environment
packages and repeated live switching. Final captures show a distinct star-lit

marks. The seven surface captures were inspected; tire colors and widths differ,
but the marks still look like regular painted strips at close range.

**INFERRED:** the unchanged native topology and physics plus passing driving
regressions preserve handling beyond the sampled routes. No exhaustive proof of
every possible contact or camera angle is claimed. **UNVERIFIED:** the limits
above, especially authenticated remote EOS and human Night driving comfort.

Recommendations for a human-authorized next round:

1. **Problem:** Dirt/Grass/Mud tracks remain too uniform and mechanically repeated;
   smoke-like soft-surface particles do not strongly distinguish dust from clods.
   **Evidence:** Dirt, Grass, Mud and DeepMud native captures show uninterrupted
   parallel strips with repetitive tread; Dirt/Grass use visibly similar puffs.
   **Severity:** Medium. **Impact:** material feedback is identifiable but lacks
   convincing surface disturbance and polish. **Suggested Improvement:** vary
   width/opacity and ragged edges over distance; break soft tracks into irregular
   scuffs and give mud heavier discrete clods while keeping everything cosmetic.
   **Scope:** In Scope. **Corrective Work Type:** New Corrective Task.
2. **Problem:** repeated effects have measurable cost, and the local multiplayer
   fixture still exhibits substantial timing stalls. **Evidence:** eight-car
   throughput figures and isolated-harness headless P99 above. **Severity:** Medium.
   **Impact:** smooth multi-car presentation is not established on this workstation.
   **Suggested Improvement:** profile a controlled before/after rendered run,
   identify sampling/material-update/transparent-overdraw costs, and reduce the
   measured bottleneck without weakening surface feedback. Treat unrelated
   scheduling stalls separately if profiling shows they predate this work.
   **Scope:** In Scope for effects cost; unrelated engine/network work requires
   separate scope approval. **Corrective Work Type:** New Corrective Task.

**Recommended Next Round:** prioritize natural soft-surface track/spray treatment,
then controlled performance profiling and a direct human Night driving session.
