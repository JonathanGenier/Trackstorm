## Implemented and reproduced causes

- **Rock landings/high-centering:** intact rocks were rejected by downward
  suspension queries, while upward chassis/rock contacts bypassed the existing
  inelastic lever-arm response. A truck could remain perched with unloaded tires.
  Downward rays now accept rock support, upward rock/chassis contacts use the
  existing impulse solver with chassis friction, and genuinely stranded rock
  contact uses the existing delayed physical roll recovery. Negligible tire droop
  contact no longer repeatedly cancels that recovery. Side anti-climb response,
  impact damage and ordinary driving remain in their existing owners.
- **Repeated rear contact:** the host's paired solve was smooth, but client
  prediction replay queried frozen remote poses between 20-Hz publications.
  Remote collision proxies now extrapolate to the queried local tick using
  published velocity within the existing 24-step prediction horizon, then restore
  accepted poses. Native transforms are explicitly flushed before the query;
  extrapolation without that flush did not fix the reproduction. Authority and
  presentation remain separate. A subsequent rendered check exposed visible
  interpenetration from the delayed remote render timeline. Nearby cars now use
  the same bounded estimate and local visible offset, blending continuously back
  to buffered rendering with distance. No collision cooldown was added.
- **Debris lifetime:** first fracture captures one authoritative deadline at
  destruction tick + 600. Descendants inherit it; further hits cannot prolong it.
  Expiry clears slots before impact processing and removes native visuals, wheel
  supports and weapon targets. TD version 3 carries the deadline through live
  publications and existing admission/resume/migration envelopes. Naturally small
  authored stones are not destroyed debris and do not time out.
- Added native repeated-contact and four-peer following checks, extended the
  imported-rock fixture for slow/close/embedded/drop scenarios, and added Core
  expiry/recovery coverage. Corrected stale map-count assertions to the existing
  authored 157 roots/628 reserved slots/2,288 plants; map assets were not changed.
  Updated affected feature documentation and fast-check routing.

## Assumptions, risks and explicitly unverified areas

- Ten seconds means 600 accepted fixed simulation ticks. Client display observes
  expiry through the existing publication cadence and transport delay; it does not
  introduce a second wall-clock destruction authority.
- Same-version peers are required: the environment wire version changed to TD3.
  Mixed-build compatibility is not provided or claimed.
- Remote query extrapolation is bounded constant velocity/angular velocity; it
  cannot predict another player's future braking, input or static-world impacts.
  Authoritative correction remains necessary during abrupt changes.
  Close-contact presentation shares this limit; distant vehicles still hold the
  endpoint of buffered snapshots.
- Native recovery/landing observations include physical roll and short transient
  penetration; the requirement is stable recovery, not zero impact motion.
- Authenticated EOS/Internet sessions, independent physical devices, exported
  builds, physical controllers, subjective audio evaluation and long-duration
  production soak beyond the recorded fixtures are explicitly unverified.
- The intermediate Godot resource-handle diagnostic above remains an intermittent
  runtime risk. The independent Astra run reproduced it on the completed build;
  see the formal critique below. Its causal relationship to TS-280 is unproven,
  and no claim of an unrelated loader fix is made.

## Sustained rock-pressure correction — 2026-10-03

### Authority and confirmed cause

Jira TS-280 was read again before changing code. Its update at
`2026-10-03T11:21:27.680-0400` includes the approved requirement to settle under
low-speed pressure, preserve legitimate climbing and natural traction, and keep
throttle/reverse/steering responsive. The human clarified that the issue affects
many small and large arena rocks. There were no required children or comments.
The correction remains on `ts-280-pg` and updates existing PR #156.

The reproduced defect is a mismatch between the collision-resolved observation
and the next commanded velocity. Both adapters remove inward rock-side velocity
using the existing anti-climb response. `VehicleMovement.Step` subsequently adds
fresh drive force into the same blocked face. The next native step recovers/slides
the chassis upward on the bevel, while the returned vertical velocity can still
point down. Repeating this produces upward creep and small hops. In the recorded
stationary fixtures the wheel support remained `(0,1,0)` and the contacts were
already classified as blocking obstacles. Incorrect near-vertical wheel support
was therefore not required to reproduce this failure. There is no separate
step-climb routine involved.

Only `VehicleMovement.cs` changes production behavior: after tire forces and thrust,
each current `EnvironmentRock != 0 && StaticObstacle` contact prevents added
inward drive velocity using the existing support-plane `ResponseNormal`, relative
to the already-solved incoming momentum. Gravity and suspension follow unchanged.
There is no new state, timer, speed cutoff, cooldown, wheel-spin effect, traction
coefficient or serialization change. Tangential/outward motion and support-normal
momentum remain available; supported tops keep ordinary suspension. Existing
impact/damage processing still happens before this constraint.

### Confirmed contact cycle and narrow correction

The previous pressure correction prevented new tire force from lifting a blocked
car, but relied on the current impact contacts. Native recovery could separate
the chassis a few millimetres, remove those contacts for one or two steps, and
allow fresh inward throttle again. In the isolated cluster trace, frames 221–222
had no rock contact and commanded approximately 0.214/0.449 m/s forward; the next
contact stopped it and native recovery moved it approximately 3.4 mm backward.
This was a repeating geometry/drive cycle, not a throttle input oscillation.

Two additional observations explain the rock-specific behavior:

- `ExposedNormal` casts a horizontal ray to find the exposed union surface of
  overlapping infrastructure. On convex rocks that ray can intersect another
  facet and replace the actual native separating normal. The ledge case switched
  between sloping ray normals and nearly horizontal native normals while wheel
  support remained approximately up. Keeping native rock normals eliminated the
  repeated reversals in four of five isolated network shape cases; the cluster
  still exposed the recovery-gap cycle.
- The preceding drive correction projected faces sequentially. Two opposing
  rock-side normals could project new drive outward from an earlier face. Both
  contact-order variants of the new deterministic test failed before the joint
  solve (lateral residual approximately 0.001593 m/s). Fixing that alone did not
  remove the runtime twitch, so it was not treated as the complete root cause.

The concrete barrier/tunnel use the infrastructure path with stable exposed
faces; authored dirt uses terrain support/face classification. None receives the
rock-only drive constraint. Their matched runtime controls retained their prior
small solver motion. No general wall, dirt, tunnel or drivetrain rule changed.

Exact production changes:

- `EnvironmentContact.ExposedNormal` now receives the source collider and retains
  the native normal for identified rocks. Infrastructure still uses its union ray.
- Both native adapters sample the solved pose with zero motion and a **1 cm rock
  contact skin**, covering the existing 5 mm motion margin. Only blocking rock
  faces are appended, after actual impact resolution, with zero impact velocity
  and impulse. The query never applies its recovery displacement. Fresh geometry
  releases the constraint immediately after separation/destruction; there is no
  cached normal, timer, cooldown, new snapshot field or serialization change.
- `EnvironmentCollision.ConstrainRockDrive` chooses the closest drive velocity
  satisfying all current rock-side planes together. It preserves already-solved
  momentum, outward/tangential escape and subsequent gravity/suspension. Throttle
  demand and natural slip calculations are unchanged. Numerical feasibility
  tolerance is not a gameplay speed dead zone.
- The native fixture now records commanded horizontal velocity and longitudinal
  travel/reversal metrics. The repeated-collision gate includes a 600-frame
  full-throttle near-contact start against all five shapes in both adapters.
  Its settled interval allows one physical settling excursion, while rejecting
  sustained alternation; the old vertical-pressure assertions remain intact.

The native-normal diagnostic flag used during isolation was removed. No runtime
debug switches, fake traction/slip effects or visual smoothing were added.
