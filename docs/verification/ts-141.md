# TS-141 — Landing-recovery collision damage forgiveness

## Scope and implementation

Read Jira TS-141 and its comment, TS-75's approved acceptance/delivery decision,
repository workflow/standards/critique, and vehicle, simulation, networking and
oval documentation. TS-141 has no required children. Branch `ts-141-pg` is based
on current main `5aa629d`; canonical Story version is `0.1.36`.

Core owns the Airborne → Recovery/Crash → Recovered/Secondary Crash episode.
Both physics adapters report explicit terrain identity, local contact position
and terrain wheel support. Forgiven contacts are removed before strongest-contact
damage selection without consuming cooldown or emitting damage events. Obstacles
and vehicles remain eligible. Damage math, HP, attribution, lifecycle and movement
forces were not changed.

Episode state is bounded, restored and replicated through aggregate version 3
and vehicle network version 8. Retuning and terminal-match snapshot reconstruction
preserve it; new lives/destruction clear it. Prediction retains confirmed episode
state and continues its existing movement-only role.

Road scene/baker and infield import changes add collision metadata only. TS-75
terrain geometry, Blender source, generator and geometry manifest are unchanged;
TS-142 retains geometry retuning scope. Pre-existing local edits to InputButtons.cs
and grass_variation.png.import are excluded from delivery.

## Runtime observations and tuning evidence

**VERIFIED:** both unchanged TS-75 jump corridors land in descending dirt zones
and recover at all six target-speed runs. Longest flights were approximately
0.87/1.07/1.30 s, with landing distances 55.40/59.80/66.17 m along the corridor.
The existing infield fixture remained at 100/100 HP. These use logical input and
native solving, without moving the car along a prescribed trajectory.
[Rendered 18 m/s landing](ts-141/west-landing.png).

**VERIFIED:** controlled drops use production 1000 HP/scale 5, eight metres above
the west landing zone and initial 12 m/s downward velocity. Upright, sideways
8 m/s travel, 3 rad/s yaw spin, banked, 35°/45° roll and 25°/35° pitch cases all
finished at 1000 HP through both adapters. Native upright bottom-out reached
14.74 m/s severity; network 45° roll reached 16.31 m/s. Both were harmless.
These probes support retaining the 50° roll/40° pitch local envelope, one-second
window and six stable support ticks; they do not establish subjective human feel
at every boundary. [Recovered spin](ts-141/native-spin.png).

**VERIFIED:** direct side/roof/front/rear drops damaged at the first meaningful
terrain contact. Practice final HP was 938.28/935.47/943.02/938.92; network final HP
was 938.43/937.53/940.95/941.01. [Roof outcome](ts-141/native-roof.png).
The tumble fixture waits for forgiven body contact, then applies a zero-HP
off-centre impulse. Both adapters recorded two forgiven contact frames followed
by Crash and 900 HP. This is a controlled secondary tumble, not a claim of a
human catching a terrain edge during ordinary driving.

**VERIFIED:** airborne obstacle-top and vehicle-top impacts damaged through both
adapters (about 43–44 HP and 42 HP respectively). Wall/containment impacts are
covered by the oval suite. Forgiveness/cooldown/attribution/mixed-contact behavior
also has direct Core checks. Scoring/event-log code is unchanged and the full
Core suite passed; no dedicated new player-visible scoring session was performed.

## Limitations and remaining risks

- Human keyboard/controller playtesting, subjective chase-camera feel, audio
  listening, remote devices, exported builds and authenticated EOS are unverified.
- The network driving harness validates normal replication under impairment; it
  does not drive remote players through every jump/crash. The landing harness
  exercises each production collision adapter locally, not Internet play.
- The separate-process run had one large startup correction during a measured
  scheduling stall. Its p99 gate passed; zero visible snaps are not claimed.
- The eight-peer stale-packet assertion failed once and passed in isolation. Loss
  of its one unreliable injected packet is consistent with the impairment, but
  actual delivery was not traced. The original failure remains recorded.
- TS-75's pre-existing uphill stop/restart defect is outside this Story. The full
  infield suite is not claimed as passing; only named jump cases ran.
- Terrain identity is authoring metadata. Future driveable surfaces must opt in;
  untagged surfaces conservatively retain ordinary collision damage.
- No new third-party assets/dependencies or changes to terrain geometry, physics
  forces or prediction architecture.
