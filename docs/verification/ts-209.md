## Root cause and choice of correction

The former 18-command allowance conflated the full outstanding-input round trip with a short speculation budget. Input transit, the bounded host queue, publication quantization and missing/reordered return snapshots all contribute. In TS-121's worst 0.8620 m episode, authority acknowledged inputs 17353 through 17356 during four client holds while client acknowledgement remained 17340. The client had 19–22 outstanding commands; a fresh snapshot then released the hold and caused the correction. This is acknowledgement starvation despite continuing authority, not a steadily growing host input queue.

Source inspection confirms that host bursts trim to six, consumption never accelerates simulation, unrecoverable gaps retire after three ticks and silence becomes neutral without inventing acknowledgements. Input redundancy repairs ordinary dropped/reordered packets. Snapshots publish every third fixed tick; loss recovery waits for newer snapshots. Receive pumps reconcile only their newest accepted movement boundary, flushing before reliable outcomes. Therefore return publication gaps can exhaust prediction even when input delivery and consumption are healthy. An acknowledgement alone cannot retire replay input safely without its matching authoritative state.

The deterministic schedule produces nine lost snapshots out of 500 (three completed clusters), 29 input drops, and delivered stale reordering. It is a fixed regression schedule, not a claim to reproduce native randomness or exact millisecond jitter. Historical TS-121 24-step evidence was diagnostic only; the above reproduction and failed queue alternative establish why headroom directly addresses the mechanism. Increasing publication frequency would add traffic and still not guarantee delivery. Adaptive growth would complicate the outage bound. A fixed 24-command allowance covers the observed 19–23 outstanding-input demand while preserving existing authority and traffic policies.

The cost is up to six additional speculative commands and collision replays; maximum unconfirmed movement is 400 ms. It is deliberately finite and cannot increase during an outage. No claim is made that arbitrary loss bursts or scheduler stalls will remain below it.
