## Implemented terrain

- Editable `InfieldTerrain.blend`, exported metre-scale GLB and original-art
  manifests retain the exact TS-74 route manifest, tunnel and obstacle locations.
- Continuous terrain includes broad hills, valleys, side berms, six southern
  rollers, four pond basins, two integrated dirt jumps and soft dirt/grass colors.
- Each jump retains the original 30/12/13/22/23 m approach/kicker/flight/landing/
  recovery reservation. A shallow tabletop beneath the flight path permits slow
  crossing. The kicker rises 2.4 m; the descending landing starts at 2 m.
- All 916 original inner-rim vertices are retained. A 28 m collar continues the
  bank grade inward and eases into the terrain. This broadens the terrain blend
  without moving the approved routes or the authoritative road geometry.
- The original flat infield visual is hidden and its active collider removed,
  allowing negative basins without overlapping support. The road, grid, external
  environment, pickups and production vehicle dimensions remain unchanged.
- The native fixture checks lane support/slopes, basin depth, jump landing
  locations, recovery, and both directions of bank access. `-Case <prefix>`
  permits isolated driving scenarios; an empty filter runs the full suite.

Blender output contains 201,521 vertices and 402,124 triangles. The dense mesh is
static imported collision, not runtime-generated terrain. No new third-party
assets, water simulation, obstacle structures or elevated bridge route are added.

## Iteration evidence

Blender 5.2.2 LTS and Godot 4.7.2 .NET were used on Windows. Visual checks use the
GTX 1070 Compatibility renderer. The scaled 4.81 m production `VehicleBody` runs
through Core and native Godot physics with logical inputs; bodies are initialized
at scenario starts and never moved artificially along routes.

Initial failures were corrected before final verification: reversed terrain face
winding, slow-speed impact in the first jump connecting profile, an early 14 m/s
landing, and missing imported vertex-color shading. High-frequency vertex-color
variation was removed after rendered inspection showed banding. The bank collar
was broadened from 20 to 28 m during native transition testing.

The oval fixture's old final-second world-Y settling assertion assumed a flat
floor. It now measures motion normal to the queried terrain, requires continuous
grounding, limits angular speed to 3 rad/s, normal rebound to 1.5 m/s (less than
12 cm equivalent free rebound under gravity), and final-second normal speed to
0.3 m/s while traversing curved ground. This is a changed terrain-specific
acceptance bound, not evidence that the original flat-floor assertion passed.
Imported visual triangles are reordered and rounded by Godot, so the geometry
check compares full spatial vertex coverage within 1 mm and equal triangle
counts rather than incorrectly requiring identical index order.

The offline `BuildOvalScene.gd` bake completed successfully with a rendering
backend. Its output contained the hidden foundation floor and terrain instance.
The equivalent compact authored scene was retained to avoid unrelated generated
resource-ID churn; the committed scene is the one exercised by runtime checks.

## Known blocker and explicit limits

The isolated `BasinRecovery-85` case starts on approximately a 2° uphill grade.
After settling, forward throttle does not engage drive because tiny negative
longitudinal velocity repeatedly selects opposing-motion braking. At the recorded
stall, position was `(-105.336, 0.627, 28.044)`, forward Y was `0.0352`, all four
wheel compressions were approximately `0.097–0.099 m`, speed was near zero, and
HP remained 100/100. This is supported native geometry, not a missing collider
or a steep terrain wall. Source inspection identifies the braking-before-drive
branch in existing `VehicleMovement`; no Core change was authorized.

The full infield suite must therefore be reported as failing when it reaches
this case. Moving route traversal and successful landings do not establish that
a player can stop and restart on every uphill surface. The Story is not fully
acceptance-ready while this recovery blocker remains.

Human keyboard/controller playtesting, subjective control feel, all off-route
stop/restart locations, opposing high-speed traffic, dense combat, authenticated
EOS, remote devices, exported builds and audio listening are not verified here.
No frame-time benchmark or low-end performance claim is made. Automated cameras
provide rendered evidence; they do not prove the ordinary chase camera is free
of every tunnel/terrain occlusion. Water gameplay and later structures remain
outside this terrain Story.

## Independent reviews and delivery state

The independent [Astra Runtime Critique — Story Round 1](ts-75/astra-critique.md)
scored **6.5/10 — FAIL**. It independently reran the 16 m/s west jump successfully
and reproduced the supported basin-start failure, inspected the rendered evidence,
and identified limited visual relief readability. The expanded crowd-impressing
spectacle criterion remains unverified. The critique distinguishes world-space
height from clearance over local terrain and does not treat damage-free runs as
the jump-design target. No corrective round or Core fix is authorized.

The independent [engineering review](ts-75/engineering-review.md) returned
**CHANGES REQUIRED** for the known recovery acceptance blocker. It identified
no new material terrain implementation defect. Its nonblocking harness finding
is that an unknown `-Case` prefix can skip all driving and still print success;
every case reported here used a known prefix and recorded actual driving evidence.

The terrain implementation is committed on `ts-75-pg` and pushed to the single
[draft PR #87](https://github.com/JonathanGenier/Trackstorm/pull/87). It is not
represented as a completed or merge-ready Story. Jira retains the recovery
blocker and the user's terrain-only constraint. The pre-existing user edits are
excluded. Delivery maintenance normalized terrain JSON output to LF and verified
all four hashes again so a clean Git checkout preserves manifest validity; no
terrain geometry or vehicle behavior changed after final verification.
