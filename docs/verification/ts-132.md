## Runtime evidence and iteration findings

The pre-change camera integration suite passed but a new native wall probe reproduced the missing behavior: the camera volume entered the wall. Baseline evidence: `.godot/camera-obstruction-checks/1ae0c3c27bd34e9c9c4de9ca646fe63a/obstruction.log`.

Implementation observation exposed two real issues before critique: an approximately 4.957 m single-frame corner contraction at 60 FPS, and a close-wall driving view inside/against the chassis. Bounded anticipation reduced the measured corner maximum to about 1.45 m at 60 FPS. The raised close view preserved the vehicle-side wall constraint and improved framing; the actual native-driving minimum increased from 0.839 m to 3.012 m. Tight geometry still produces a close cropped view, not a guarantee of a fully visible silhouette.

The focused geometry sequence uses scripted displayed poses and real physics queries, not a simulated full driving route. It covers a tall wall approach/hold/clear, thin barrier and low orbit, building corner rotation, moving frozen rigid-body prop, slope, repeated edge crossings, shake beside a wall, new life/vehicle/resume/reconstruction, production yard boundary/container/barrier, the production oval's concave bank, an initially overlapping target, enlarged near plane and a low ceiling. Static hold settles below 1 mm movement/frame; clearing recovers monotonically. The real-driving sequence separately uses actual input frames, production VehicleBody/VehicleArena, native contacts, orbit and real reset.

The first final recording lost synthetic input when focus changed and correctly failed its requirement that the production barrier actually affect the camera. Both recorder harnesses now explicitly hold their synthetic input gate open; focus/suppression behavior itself is still verified by the existing camera suite. An initial 144 FPS rate-scaled corner assertion failed at 0.636 m against a 0.625 m threshold; the guard was adjusted to 100 m/s (1.667 m/frame at 60 FPS) to allow exponential discretization while still rejecting the original multi-metre snap. Actual maxima remain recorded; this bound is a regression guard, not a subjective polish score.

## Assumptions, limitations and risks

Existing layer-1 colliders define solid world geometry. Decorative meshes without collision are intentionally ignored; no camera-specific gameplay collision was authored. Native initial-overlap recovery is bounded, not a guarantee for arbitrary invalid vehicle teleports deep inside sealed geometry. Abrupt teleports/newly arriving geometry can require immediate safe contraction. Very cramped spaces can crop the car despite the bounded lift; there is no vehicle fading, spectator camera or broader chase retune.

Recordings use fixed frame rates and synthetic controls. They support chronological visual inspection, not prolonged human comfort, a real-time performance benchmark or every aspect ratio/hardware combination. The geometry fixture includes real production assets but moves the displayed pose directly; real driving, networking and lifecycle integration are separate checks. Multiplayer coverage is local UDP with injected latency/jitter/loss; authenticated EOS, remote devices and hardware gamepads were not exercised. Obstruction-specific blocked-camera reconnect is inferred from the direct reset test plus the actual reused-camera reconnect test, not independently staged as a blocked multiplayer reconnection.

No changes to Core, authority, vehicle forces, HP/input frames, collision layers or network payloads are present in the Story diff. Passing native replay/integration checks supports that boundary; it does not claim exhaustive physics equivalence under every possible scene.
