## Implemented result

- Core consumes the existing authoritative physical contact and retains the exact
  vehicle/life-local position and normal. Every claw has its own identity and
  original 1,800-tick deadline. New hits neither refresh earlier claws nor multiply
  their penalty.
- Existing vehicle movement scales engine/reverse drive and ground/air Nitro
  thrust to 20%. The final expiry/removal restores power at that fixed boundary.
  Steering, service braking, health, external impulses and existing momentum keep
  their ordinary rules. Tire traction still bounds realized acceleration.
- Complete item publications, fresh-admission checkpoints, reconnect checkpoints,
  authority recovery and movement prediction carry the appropriate membership or
  deadline. Target death/new life, explicit reset, final departure and match finish
  clear attachments. Transient suspension keeps original timers advancing.
- Production presentation follows the same rendered vehicle transform as the car:
  four-jaw grip, impact sparks, inflated red nose, local electricity and a shared
  vehicle effect. Each natural expiry deflates, fizzles, releases and falls away.
  Attached/in-flight EMP identities and cosmetic tails are bounded; the shared
  vehicle effect uses 96 core and 96 halo segments regardless of claw count.
- `RemoveEmpAttachment(id)` is a host-only, idempotent queued-removal seam for a
  future caller. No nose collision/damage, shield counterplay or stored-ammunition
  destruction was implemented. Standard Missile behavior was not changed.
- Item protocol TI30, movement version 14 (177 bytes), and discovery bucket 22
  reject incompatible layouts. Story versioning, workflow policies, documentation
  indexes, assets and dependencies were left unchanged as requested.

## Direct runtime evidence and limitations

**VERIFIED:** The final attachment harness ran three real native UDP peers with
100 ms configured delay, 15 ms jitter and 3% loss. Each peer observed ten launches
and ten impacts. Six rotated native collision faces retained their actual local
anchors. Three simultaneous claws had deadlines 3364, 3442 and 3520; each ended
independently and the final reported power fraction was 1. Maximum measured
rendered-anchor error over 180 moving/rolling fixture frames was 0.000022909284 m.

The harness then released scripted victim poses, supplied throttle/turn and normal
Nitro use, and seeded one airborne angular-velocity fixture before allowing
production movement/native collision queries to integrate the tumble. Reported
displacement was 66.66677 m; final HP 978.46106 includes physical collision damage.
Separate deterministic contact/expiry tests confirm EMP itself adds no damage.
Every peer reclaimed attachment/release instances. The final native run exited
without resource warnings.

**VERIFIED:** Current final captures under `.godot/emp-attachment-checks/` were
visually inspected: side, roof, underside, three attachments, natural expiry,
`native-driving-nitro.png`, and `native-tumble-close.png`. They show contact-local
noses/grips, blue arcs during driving/tumbling and deflation at expiry. The
JSON results retain anchors, deadlines, network counts and measured error.

**VERIFIED:** Core tests cover independent deadlines, same-boundary removal/expiry,
malformed and duplicate state, atomic failure, lethal damage/respawn, departure,
suspension/resume, complete serialization, fresh-admission checkpoints and restored
host continuation. Engine/reverse and ground/air Nitro force comparisons retain
20%; braking is unchanged. Prediction clears on the exact deadline.

**INFERRED:** Complete checkpoint tests and production publication integration
support live late-join/reconnect continuity. The new native harness creates and
serializes a real admission checkpoint and reconstructs current presentation;
it does not disconnect/rejoin a fourth EMP-affected native peer. The broader
reconnect harness stopped at its pre-existing Wrench prerequisite.

**UNVERIFIED:** Human keyboard/controller driving feel, subjective audio, remote
Internet/authenticated EOS sessions, cross-machine active-EMP reconnect, exports,
64-claw stress, sustained GPU/memory profiling, and the ten-minute transport soak.
Automated input, pose fixtures and inspected still captures are not human
playtesting or a frame-time benchmark. No new third-party assets were introduced.

Human playtest access remains Developer Options → Configs → Item spawns: set
`spawns.emp_claw_weight` positive, all other item weights zero, Weapon category
positive, then collect an ordinary pickup. Default deployment/stow/interval are
1.2 seconds, 0.82 seconds and 50 ticks. Use the normal weapon controls against an
unshielded rival. Nose-pop and shield-counterplay gameplay remain future scope.
