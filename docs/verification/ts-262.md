# TS-262 — Machine Gun camera-aiming integration

## Scope and implementation

Implemented on `ts-262-jg`. Jira TS-262 and TS-234 were read on October 7, 2026; both had zero comments and TS-262 had no children. Both descriptions explicitly approve implementing against the current placeholder before TS-234. The remaining TS-234 blocker link is stale. No production model, new firing VFX, tracer redesign, impact VFX, targeting, cursor or assistance system was introduced.

- Machine Gun firing requires the living selected capability's ready accepted solution for the current pre-command simulation tick. It uses that solution's origin and direction, with the existing deterministic per-token/per-round cone samples expressed about accepted direction.
- Missing/expired aim, deployment and blocked center-ray clearance pause ammunition/phase advancement. There is no forward fallback or deferred burst. Existing sustained activation, release, selection, death, recovery, damage, cadence, capacity, falloff, knockback and tracer cadence remain owned by their existing systems.
- The accepted rack pivot is the placeholder's virtual muzzle. Existing yaw mount and pitched payload already follow the shared solution beneath the fixed rack. No model-specific muzzle offset is invented. TS-234 owns future production geometry and its revalidation.
- Spread samples that intersect the conservative owner envelope stop as cover without self damage. These actual rounds still spend ammo at normal cadence. Closer native cover or shields retain closest-hit ordering. Published endpoints use the same accepted origin on every peer.
- Existing mechanical/weapon fixtures now provide fresh aim and wait for deployment instead of assuming immediate chassis-forward firing. The shared native aiming fixture now validates actual camera-directed bursts, multiple sustained shooters, remote outcomes and presentation, expiry, suppression and switching before its existing camera/lifecycle matrix.

## Runtime evidence and iteration findings

**VERIFIED:** Native rendered bursts hit and damage targets forward, to the side, behind, elevated in the air and alongside. The observer's yaw/pitch settles to accepted aim, the rack stays fixed, and the payload's scale/pivot agree with the shared attachment. Two simultaneous shooters and ordered identical remote shot endpoints passed in the integrated fixture. Rendered [side fire](ts-262-evidence/mg-side.png), [rear fire](ts-262-evidence/mg-rear.png), [airborne target](ts-262-evidence/mg-airborne.png), [close-side fire](ts-262-evidence/mg-close-side.png) and [simultaneous sustained fire](ts-262-evidence/mg-multiple-sustained.png) were inspected. Existing placeholder/VFX limitations remain visible.

The rear fixture initially placed its target beyond the prototype perimeter at z=50. Direct camera-ray diagnostics found the intervening wall; the corrected rear target is at z=45. No production camera behavior was changed. A reset/input ordering error in the new self-clearance fixture was corrected by allowing reset to complete before injecting downward motion.

The older native Machine Gun fixture's chassis-center damage-pressure threshold no longer describes rack-origin geometry. It now checks damage against independently accumulated native hits and the unchanged falloff formula, while requiring meaningful near-range hits, reduced far reliability, range/cover rejection and the same ammo timing. In the impaired run, host close fire hit 50/161 rounds (112.48 HP), far fire hit 3/481 (1.42 HP), remote close fire hit 33/154 (74.24 HP), and all committed scoring agreed across peers. This is evidence of changed geometric coverage, not a claim of unchanged hit probability.

## Assumptions, risks and explicitly unverified areas

- Placeholder art intentionally has no production barrel/muzzle. Its pivot is the functional muzzle; production-model articulation/offset/clearance is TS-234 scope.
- The shared cursor indicates camera intent and readiness, not perfect bullet alignment. Spread, articulation limits, close parallax, native cover and network delay remain observable. Near the downward limit, some spread hits the owner's conservative envelope and uses the existing impact feedback without self damage.
- Synthetic input, authored target positions and local real UDP establish native integration, not physical-controller ergonomics, prolonged human play, WAN/multi-device behavior or authenticated EOS reconnection.
- No new protocol, serialization, host tuning or camera preference fields were added. Camera settings retain their existing owner and rendered-ray integration.
