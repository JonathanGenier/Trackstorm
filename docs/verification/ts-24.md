## Implementation and changed areas

- **Core vehicles/simulation:** `VehicleLifecycle`, `RespawnConfiguration`, `VehicleSnapshot`, `VehicleAuthority` and `Simulation` extend the existing vehicle aggregate. Core owns participation, the lethal boundary, absolute respawn deadline, full reset and immutable committed lifecycle hooks.
- **Core arena/items:** `ArenaConfiguration` selects validated markers and reserves simultaneous respawns. `ItemAuthority` gates use/grants, clears inventory by default, supports configured retention with fresh tokens, and retires dead owners' missiles. `ItemSpawnAuthority` rejects inactive pickup claims before consuming availability or selecting an item.
- **Core replication:** host input buffers and prediction history retire old-life controls without breaking acknowledgements. Prediction advances movement without deciding HP or lifecycle. Aggregate codec v2 and vehicle wire protocol v3 carry lifecycle/deadline; inputs identify their observed life.
- **Client networking/presentation:** the existing driver publishes lifecycle boundaries reliably, independently of ordinary movement cadence and item revisions. Bodies, remote interpolation, correction smoothing and camera reconstruct the accepted life. `VehicleDestructionEffects` uses existing Kenney CC0 fire/smoke/spark assets, once per death, with bounded cleanup. The online compatibility bucket advances to `trackstorm-lobby-3`.
- **Tests/docs:** deterministic lifecycle tests, replication/driver regression tests, an eight-peer native death harness, `check-death-respawn.ps1`, and the relevant `features.md` sections. The arena verification fixture now traverses obstacles over multiple bounded steps; its previous 600 m/s single-step expectation contradicted the production 65 m/s clamp.

Core remains engine-independent with the production dependency direction `Client -> Core`. No Shared layer, parallel health/inventory authority, new dependency, dedicated death-animation system, TS-26 scoring or TS-35 audio was introduced. Existing asset provenance and licenses cover all reused particle textures.

## Assumptions, limitations and risks

- Dead is the lethal boundary, followed by Respawning on the next tick. A one-tick delay goes directly from Dead to Alive at the following boundary. The configured deadline is the earliest eligible respawn when marker occupancy prevents placement.
- Simultaneous living attackers may trade lethal hits in the same batch. After that boundary, inactive players and their projectiles cannot produce damage.
- Spawn clearance considers other living vehicles, not movable props or opponent line of sight. A prop moved onto an otherwise valid marker can still compromise placement. No spawn protection is implemented.
- Wire/aggregate versions changed intentionally; older builds and saved aggregate envelopes are incompatible. EOS discovery uses the new compatibility bucket.
- Native verification was on one machine. No separate-PC, WAN/NAT, authenticated multi-peer EOS death gameplay, physical-controller play session, export, long soak, or memory/frame-time profile was performed for this Story. Earlier Stories' Internet verification is not claimed as verification of these changes.
- Startup correction spikes remain an observed networking limitation; this run does not establish their cause. Death-specific tests confirmed reset offsets cleared, but general networking should not be described as highly polished.
