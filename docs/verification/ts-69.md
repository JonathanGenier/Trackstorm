Baseline immediate-input counts only proved history progress; completed counts also require predicted-tick progress without a new acknowledgement.

**Delay caveat:** baseline latest-tick-minus-cursor values can overstate actual presentation age because sampling clamps an out-of-history cursor to the oldest retained endpoint. Multi-second values establish broken clock recovery, not measured multi-second visual latency. Baseline actual clamped sample age was not recorded. Completed buffering measures a cursor inside retained data. Received-timeline delay adds snapshot age; neither includes unknown one-way transit. Half RTT is only a symmetric-path estimate. Fixed-step metrics can repeat the last presentation sample while a process stalls.

### What the new measurements exposed

The old standalone fixture forced **64** physics steps per rendered frame. The application uses Godot's ordinary **8**-step limit. Cold scene construction could therefore cause the fixture to manufacture over a second of sequenced controls during catch-up, exceed prediction's 18-command horizon, retire host input backlog and shift the authority tick/ack relationship. These are real recorded corrections under that fixture, but they are not representative of ordinary application scheduling. The production scheduling policy was not changed in this round.

On synchronized main `fa01877`, the unchanged 64-step eight-player baseline had a 1.984 m worst startup correction and a 2.287 m worst steady correction. The latter occurred after recorded damage. A scheduling-only comparison, before the allocation optimization, reduced the worst startup to 0.406 m and worst steady to 0.188 m, with zero hard snaps. One client held prediction for two steady ticks. These separate unseeded trials establish a substantial fixture effect, not a precise causal percentage or a production-code performance gain.

Temporary diagnostic timing around native sweep, suspension and water observation found occasional 10–20 ms observation stalls, but does not distinguish CPU work from OS descheduling. That timing instrumentation was removed from production code after diagnosis. Its bounded records remain in `diagnostic-100` and `production-scheduler-eight`; the temporary instrumentation itself was not committed. The shipped fixture retains step timing, managed allocation counts and correction details at **10 cm** (previously 1 m).

The old rendered fixture also performed GPU readback and PNG encoding at 3.5 driving seconds on the gameplay thread. In two repeated pre-final-integration trials, ~189/192 ms frames followed this capture, with correction events immediately afterward (up to 0.243 m). With capture moved after measurements, the first rendered comparison recorded zero steady corrections and no steady frame stall above 100 ms; its 138.640 ms startup frame remains disclosed. `-CaptureDuringDriving` retains the old behavior and measures capture duration explicitly. This is a measurement repair, not a claim that image capture performance was optimized.

At the same eight-step setting with the old mid-drive capture still enabled, the paired rendered allocation comparison was **37,962.43 → 35,310.48 managed bytes per client step** (about 7% lower) and **0.682 → 0.646 ms** mean client step time. Host allocation was **118,222.44 → 113,401.55 bytes/step**. Adaptive driving and OS timing make these observations, not an isolated microbenchmark; they do not prove elimination of GC or scheduling stalls. Raw values are in `baseline-rendered` and `candidate-rendered`.
