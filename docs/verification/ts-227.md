## Implemented

- Exhaust presentation now consumes actual nonnegative elapsed time instead of capping each update at 100 ms. Previously a 700 ms rendering gap advanced release/depletion by only 100 ms; now a gap spanning the remaining tail expires combustion and new particle emission on the next update. The camera already used elapsed time. Normal-frame timing, flame design, resource use and propulsion are unchanged.
- Extended the native oval fixture to seven integrated scenarios: activation/sustain/release, re-engagement during release, steering/traffic, high-speed unboosted driving, airborne Boost/landing, sub-priming taps and three simultaneous boosters. It measures joint exhaust/camera cutoff, cancelled ignition, simultaneous flame coverage and finite bounds, and records exhaust energy plus camera/body poses.
- Added an optional interactive fixture with toggle controls for directly exercising Boost, steering, reset and release. This uses native bodies and production presentation with automatic throttle and verification-staged Nitro; it is not the production inventory/input UI.
- The actual two-peer UDP Nitro fixture now keeps both production local chase cameras current and checks their sustained response/recovery alongside charge, release, reuse, confirmed depletion and local/remote exhaust. Removed its separate overview camera.
- Added a rendered slow-frame shutdown regression for both release and depletion. Exhaust changes now route the integrated camera fixture through the fast-check tools. Current camera/exhaust feature documentation and verification index were updated.
- Corrected the older native vehicle silhouette assertion to measure the authored Car independently of the persistent booster payload's hidden flame meshes; those VFX had inflated the measured length to 9.078 m. No geometry was altered.
- Synchronized Story version to 0.2.7 / Windows preset 0.2.7.0 against current main `39b8be2`. No Core, physics, input, network schema, inventory, HUD or third-party asset changes.

## Assumptions, limitations and explicitly unverified areas

- Short natural smoke/flame tails are intentional accepted presentation, not extra authoritative thrust. Depletion is confirmed by inventory; recovery speed alone must not ignite effects.
- The accepted subtle TS-226 camera response is retained. Boost is more visually pronounced than ordinary driving through the combined exhaust, FOV and peripheral wind cues; this is not a renewed authorization for aggressive camera motion.
- No remote Internet/EOS, separate-PC, 8-player network session, low-end GPU, ultrawide, audio-listening, physical-controller ergonomics or prolonged human-comfort assessment was performed. Two local UDP peers and 32 scripted render vehicles are explicitly different evidence categories.
- Depletion/resource semantics use real item authority in the two-peer fixture. Oval scripted/interactive driving stages movement requests and selected rack state, and cannot substitute for resource-authority verification.
- GPU particles remaining from earlier emissions are allowed to dissipate naturally. The hitch regression checks CPU presentation state/emission deadlines; it does not simulate a real GPU stall or prove particle aging under a frozen driver.
- High-speed rolled-car obstruction remains unresolved. Normal gameplay framing is readable in inspected samples, but the feature cannot yet be described as uniformly polished across collisions.
