## Persistent Oil

Oil uses the existing vehicle, split trunk, hydraulic ram and weapon rack. The [original Blender asset](../../assets/items/oil/README.md) supplies a closed square red/ivory box, hinged lid, metal spring and rear-facing jester. Selection raises the closed box through the existing mechanism; the lid then opens and the spring extends automatically. Bounded fixed-step inertia reacts to acceleration, braking, corners and vertical motion. Early local use queues only the confirmed life/token/selection capability until this payload is ready; switching, discard, death and recovery invalidate that queued intent.

One activation consumes one slot and creates one logical `OilPatch`. Gameplay tire coverage is queried in Core; cosmetic geometry never adds a native vehicle-blocking collider. Its captured `OilPour` emits for one second by default (40 parcels at fractional 1.5-tick intervals). Projectile origins follow the accepted moving head socket; launch stays chassis-rearward independently of spring tilt. `OilJet` raises its ballistic elevation only as needed to clear the box, rack and rear body from the moving mouth, including a fluid-envelope margin. At unusually slow/high-gravity tuning, the rearward component has a clearance floor. It never moves the box or projects an impact onto the ground. Parcels inherit vehicle velocity, integrate gravity at 60 Hz and sweep each full segment against static world geometry. A shot in flight deposits on its first actual supported impact, never beneath the car automatically. Static banks, ramps and walls retain the impact normal. Water impacts are discarded. At ledges/nonplanar contacts, the center and four 64-point support rings reduce the footprint or discard it; the interior rings detect narrow ledges that an outer perimeter can miss. Vehicle collision proxies are excluded from the Oil sweep: a car cannot become permanent world-space support, and parcels continue to the real surface. Moving/animated bodies, movable wall/rock layers, destructible rocks and shots starting embedded in geometry retire without retaining Oil. Only stationary world support can retain a patch. Parcels retire after three seconds or beyond the fixed 4096 m world bounds.

Core retains emission timing, source life, captured physics/spring parameters and immutable flight samples. One vehicle cannot start another pour before its accepted firing interval ends; reaching the hazard budget retains the held capability. Death/reset/departure cancels emission while already-fired parcels and deposited segments remain match-owned. Temporary disconnect and complete recovery preserve continuation. Reliable flight samples publish at 10 Hz; Client extrapolates at most 100 ms. Contact latches and expiration removal publish immediately. This remains the existing item channel, with its delayed-delivery limitations.

The mouth opens for firing, then closes over twelve accepted ticks before head retraction, lid closure and rack descent. A bottom-anchored coil follows the collar throughout. Accepted kickback moves the head forward/up without turning aim. Pickup replacement retains its capability while this sequence completes; rapid selections coalesce. Reconstruction uses the accepted pour clock without replaying use. Cosmetic ready-phase spring/deployment progress is not serialized.

Live lifetime edits apply to future deployments and never refresh existing deadlines.

## Tire contamination, spray and trails

`TireFeedback` reconstructs independent cosmetic contamination from each tire’s accepted movement timer. A newly observed entry seeds up to six seconds of contamination; repeated snapshots and continuous overlap cannot refresh it. Recovery/reconstruction seeds the remaining cosmetic interval without replaying a historical splash. Cosmetic history already older than the handling timer is intentionally not sent to a fresh participant. Death/new life clears coatings and emission immediately.

Each affected rubber mesh receives its own dark glossy material overlay. Four fixed 32-particle oil emitters per vehicle produce small wet droplets on supported contact; speed and axle slip increase spray density and velocity while diminishing coverage reduces them. Droplets have a 0.55-second lifetime, no collision and no gameplay callback. Emitters, meshes, materials and coatings are released with the vehicle.

Individual trails sample the articulated wheel contact path at the existing maximum 20 Hz cadence. Dark, low-roughness strips share the arena’s fixed 65,536-slot tire-mark ring and its local graphics budget, width, intensity, quality and distance controls. Oil marks last twelve seconds, fading over the last eight. Diminishing tire coverage also reduces newly emitted alpha. Trail sampling requires native supported ground, excludes water and airborne wheels, and breaks at missing support, sharp normal changes, teleports and life changes. A midpoint probe rejects unsupported spans or surface discontinuities; landing starts a fresh path. Existing oil strips survive removal of either the car or originating hazard until their own bounded expiry. Cosmetic sampling never writes item state, creates hazards, triggers contacts or awards points.
