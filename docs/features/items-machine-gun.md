# Items Machine Gun

Read the [shared system contract](items.md) first. This reference contains only the selected subsystem; preserve its shared ownership and integration requirements.

## Sustained Machine Gun resource

Every newly acquired Machine Gun starts with the configured discrete ammunition capacity (800 rounds by default) in its physical held slot. Holding **UseItem** after an accepted capability-bound use sustains fire; releasing, switching slots, losing input ownership or disconnecting stops further shots while preserving the exact remaining rounds and fractional firing phase. At the default 80 rounds/second (4,800 RPM), 800 rounds deplete in approximately 10 seconds of actual firing. Exhaustion clears only that physical slot through the ordinary item lifecycle.

The host consumes the current ready [accepted weapon aim](weapon-aiming.md#machine-gun-firing-consumer), adds deterministic spread and queries the Client host's closest native hit over the full pivot-to-range segment. Missing, expired, deploying or self-blocked aim pauses ammunition consumption without catch-up fire. Static geometry and owner-envelope intersections stop the ray; valid living rival hits stage damage and impulse through the existing authority transaction. Defaults remain 225 m range, falloff beginning at 12 m, 2.25 HP per round and 8 N·s knockback. The fixed 0.42-degree cone half-angle targets approximately 80% frame inclusion at 200 m; frame inclusion is distinct from native collision and damage. There is no homing, forced hit quota or visual resampling. Exponent-1.5 falloff reaches zero at 225 m. Perfect close-range hits total 1800 HP per default magazine against a 1000 HP vehicle. Applied rival HP loss uses the shared Circus scoring authority.

Confirmed impacts retain six short-lived orange sparks at the accepted hit point, independently of tracer frequency. The existing burst cap bounds catch-up work; particles fade in 0.22 seconds and their nodes retire at 0.3 seconds. They scatter back along the incoming ray because publication does not carry a surface normal. Impact presentation is independent of the travelling streak pool.

The HUD shows authoritative remaining magazine percentage and never predicts ammunition. Remaining rounds, acquisition capacity, fractional firing phase and actual rounds fired serialize in the existing item protocol and survive fresh admission, reconnect/resume and host migration without replaying historical shots. Developer Options expose capacity, ammo units per round, aggregate fire rate, range, damage, falloff start/exponent, spread, knockback and per-barrel tracer frequency.

## Bullet and tracer presentation

`MachineGunFirePresentation` owns one arena-local MultiMesh with 512 preallocated instances and a 64-flight cap per shooter, including pending first-frame emissions. It creates no per-round nodes, meshes or materials. Saturation drops new cosmetic flights without queueing a replay burst. Ordinary rounds draw short gold 1.1 m streaks; colored tracers draw brighter 4.2 m streaks with a narrow, distance-capped width. Each gun independently repeats **gold, gold, colored**: left uses red, right uses green. Aggregate shot ordinals 0/1 and 2/3 are gold; 4 is red and 5 is green, then the sequence repeats. The third bullet is replaced by a tracer, never supplemented by another projectile. The host's tunable per-gun interval defaults to three; it selects every Nth actual round of that gun. Release/reuse and recovery preserve each magazine's ordinal.

Gold opacity/intensity is increased and colored tracers use a stronger separate intensity. Alpha blending preserves color against bright surfaces without additive washout. The procedural shader tapers both ends and softens the width; it adds no lights, bloom or oversized bullet spheres.

Network receipt reserves a bounded pool slot but does not freeze an out-of-date muzzle. At render priority 100, after arena pose interpolation, rack yaw/pitch and recoil, each new flight captures its actual individual barrel endpoint exactly once. The pool uses world transforms with engine physics interpolation disabled, matching the arena's manual presentation. Flights then move at a cosmetic 600 m/s toward the unchanged confirmed endpoint. They are clipped at that endpoint and expire after at most 550 ms, including a displaced local presentation pose. Damage and impact timing remain immediate authoritative outcomes; these are not new gameplay projectiles. No extra spread, target selection, native ray or ammunition consumption enters presentation. A zero-length accepted segment means cover before the muzzle plane and produces no outgoing streak. A segment whose endpoint is behind its cosmetic origin is also suppressed.

Core publishes `ItemEvent.MachineGunRound`, the actual zero-based shot ordinal, and both the rig and flight pool use its alternating barrel identity. Each event represents one real authoritative ray and one damage/consumption transaction; the second gun is not a duplicate visual of another round. The default is 80 rounds/s across the assembly (40 per gun), with one ammo unit spent per round. Shared aiming, deterministic spread, full protected collision segments, damage and falloff are unchanged. Batched events, final depletion, either inventory slot and checkpoint replacement retain the same explicit sequence, independent of rendering or ammo-cost edits.

Each muzzle owns its own confirmed pulse, heat and shot-age envelope. Flash and smoke emit exactly at that gun's articulated endpoint; the other muzzle cannot borrow its firing timer. Release and readiness loss stop both emitters. Flights remain in world space after emission, so moving the car does not drag an old shot along. Existing flights drain within the lifetime bound. Recovery clears the pool and repeated publications cannot duplicate flights.

The barrel tips are obtained from the production rig including yaw, pitch and recoil. The paired barrels bracket the shared authoritative axis at X ±0.28896 m. Cosmetic convergence to the confirmed endpoint preserves actual spread outcomes while the complete authoritative pivot-to-range collision segment still protects nearby cover. The fixed vehicle rack and Core firing rules retain their existing owners.

`check-machine-gun.ps1 -GodotPath <exe>` is the feature-specific native harness for sustained host/remote firing and short-range native rays. Core tests cover cadence, depletion/reuse, falloff/attribution, invalid ray observations, tuning, two-slot state and checkpoint continuation. These are local-machine checks, not authenticated EOS/WAN/multi-device or long-session balance evidence.

`check-machine-gun-vfx.ps1 -GodotPath <exe> -Motion` renders production barrels with scripted outcomes at 30/60 FPS, checks moving/rapid-yaw/pitch first-frame origins at both frame rates, cardinal/overhead origins and endpoint clipping, and exercises eight simultaneous shooters at default and maximum configured fire rates. It records pool bounds and CPU update timing separately from GPU or network performance. `check-weapon-aim.ps1 -MachineGun -Visual -Impaired` exercises the existing real three-peer Machine Gun aiming scenarios, ordered endpoints, final pre-draw muzzle alignment and color sequence on every peer, actual driving with rapid yaw/pitch, simultaneous shooters and lifecycle stops. The full aiming harness still runs these scenarios before its wider camera checks.

## Tuning and ammunition accounting

`items.machine_gun_ammo_per_round` is a host-validated integer from 1–10, default 1. It changes consumption for future actual rounds without multiplying cadence, rays or damage. If fewer units remain than the configured cost, the last real round consumes that remainder and exhausts the item. Capacity edits affect subsequent pickups only; live magazines retain their acquired capacity and remaining amount. `MachineGunAmmo.RoundsFired` advances once per real shot, independent of consumption cost, and survives the same state/recovery path. Two physical item slots retain independent resources. Default capacity remains 800; numeric ammunition HUD and any future 500-round default are separate work.

Item protocol TI33 carries explicit event ordinals and magazine counters. Configuration TC56 carries 285 values (2301 bytes), including ammo usage. Older layouts are rejected; peers must run matching builds. Online discovery uses `trackstorm-lobby-25` to keep the preceding layouts out of compatible searches. There is no secondary Client ammunition store.

The searchable **Machine Gun · Local VFX** accordion follows existing session-local cosmetics: bullet speed (300–1000 m/s), ordinary length (0.4–2 m), tracer length (1–6 m), width (0.5–2×), gold intensity (0.25–2.5×, default 1.35), tracer intensity (1–4×, default 2.5), flash scale (0–1.5×) and smoke intensity (0–2×). Bullet edits are captured on new emissions; muzzle controls apply live. Speed and size defaults are retained; the approved brighter presentation and one-in-three per-gun sequence replace the earlier random color mix. Zero disables flash or smoke. Local Reset restores defaults; these values are neither saved nor exported nor replicated. Extreme slow/high-rate tuning can drop excess cosmetic flights at the unchanged pool/lifetime ceilings; it cannot slow or alter authoritative shots.

`check-developer-options.ps1 -MachineGunOnly -Visual` exercises discovery, all local editors, client-requested authoritative Apply, rejection, Cancel, Reset and fresh-process defaults. The rendered VFX harness verifies independent muzzle effects, actual tuning responses and exact per-gun color sequence. Native aiming verifies both barrels on every peer, while Core checks bind their alternating identities to actual damage and ammo use.
