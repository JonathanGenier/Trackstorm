# Camera-relative weapon aiming

The shared direct-fire foundation serves Machine Gun and standard Missile. Salvo keeps its separate mechanics. The production Car's deployed rack stays fixed: its child mount yaws and the child payload pitches. Existing placeholder weapon artwork is retained.

**Current integration boundary:** Machine Gun consumes the ready accepted direction and origin for authoritative fire, with deterministic spread calibrated for approximately 80% grouping inside a production car's frame at 200 m. Standard Missile still fires along its established forward direction; its weapon-specific camera-aim integration remains separate. The centered cursor represents camera intent, not a guarantee of impact or an accepted-ray reticle.

## Authority and lifecycle

`Core.Items.WeaponAim` solves chassis-relative yaw/pitch from a finite unit direction and authoritative vehicle pose. `ItemAuthority` owns transient intent and accepted solutions; `HostVehicleSession` enforces sender, generation and participation. Requests bind to current life, selected grant token, selection watermark and monotonic counter, never a claimed player or hit outcome.

The accepted contract contains owner/life/token/tick, yaw, pitch, origin/direction, clearance and readiness. Full yaw supports side/rear aim. Host tuning bounds angular rate and pitch. A tick advances articulation at most once, including retries of rejected gameplay batches. A conservative production-body ray test covers the pivot-to-exit path, so offsetting a muzzle cannot skip the owner. Self-intersecting requests clamp to a clear pose but remain not ready. The shared pivot is the deployed rack payload attachment at local `(0, 1.81, 1.845)` metres.

Readiness starts on accepted ownership/selection, independently of first camera input. A shared two-phase mechanical helper serves the existing Client rack animation and a conservative Core readiness gate. The gate integrates current trunk/rack speeds every tick, reserving initial deployment or full retraction/deployment on a capability replacement, even without a slot switch. Replacing Nitro additionally reserves its 0.24-second nozzle nesting. Capability history survives empty-slot and aim-only resets; life change, departure and recovery clear it. Client animation retains its existing smooth partial-cycle reversal rather than snapping to a conservative phase. Selection, depletion, invalid life, death, removal and suspension retire transient aim. Requests expire after 15 host ticks. No additional inventory, damage or checkpoint authority is introduced.

Aim preparation uses the exact current native observation batch passed to the item step, matching the existing weapon handlers' pre-command pose phase. If that item/world transaction throws, the host restores transient aim and readiness state before retry. Presentation remains downstream of a successful host step.

An accepted Proxy Mine placement retains ownership of the existing rack until placement and its 0.23-second arm return finish. Selecting or acquiring a direct-fire capability during that placement holds its readiness gate before the replacement path; it does not interrupt or redesign mine placement.

## Machine Gun firing consumer

Machine Gun requires the current pre-command tick's ready solution for its living selected capability. Missing, expired, deploying or self-blocked aim spends no rounds and does not accumulate a deferred burst. Sustained engagement, release/re-press, fractional cadence, capacity, damage, falloff, impulse and tracer selection retain their existing owners. Recovery preserves ammo and firing phase but requires new transient aim and deployment readiness; no vehicle-forward fallback exists.

The accepted origin at the yaw/pitch pivot is the current placeholder's virtual muzzle. Its labelled box has no production barrel or muzzle attachment. The fixed rack, yaw mount and pitched box use the existing hierarchy and full deployed scale. Presentation smooths accepted angles and retains its existing small use pulse; it does not relocate authoritative shots. Future production geometry must align its muzzle within this shared contract and revalidate clearance.

Spread uses a chassis-up tangent basis around accepted direction, retaining the same token/round deterministic samples and uniform-disk distribution. The default cone half-angle is 0.42 degrees. It is fixed angular dispersion for both framed and free fire; no target size, distance, identity or hit quota enters shot sampling. The 200 m benchmark compares actual rays at target depth with the projected body frame; frame inclusion does not grant collision or damage. Shorter distances naturally tighten grouping. Maximum range remains 225 m and damage/falloff are unchanged. Native closest-hit queries start at the accepted pivot, including nearby cover before any future muzzle offset. A spread sample can intersect the conservative owner envelope even when the center ray is clear: that fired round stops there as cover, spends ammunition at normal cadence, and does no self damage. Existing impact/tracer publication presents its endpoint. It never resamples toward a target or shoots through the owner.

## Camera, assistance and HUD

Selected direct-fire items share the close, low [chase camera](camera.md), orbiting the fully extended rack attachment used by `WeaponAim.Pivot`. The elevated boom and shallow viewing angle leave the payload below the centered cursor. The deployed reference remains stable while stowed/deploying; accepted weapon rotation never feeds back into camera orientation. The shared camera can tilt 85 degrees upward or downward, equally with and without a weapon. Upward view pitch retains the chase lens position instead of lowering the camera around the chassis; downward input retains its elevated orbit. RMB mouse movement and the remapped camera stick change desired aim. Releasing RMB always ends mouse assistance and resumes the same smooth chase return. Neutral controller input retains an eligible engagement initiated by the existing camera controls; otherwise it returns normally. Equipping a weapon introduces no separate camera pose. The shared weapon contract still supports its independently bounded chassis-relative yaw/pitch; camera controls remain constrained by the camera's view limits.

Mouse displacement remains direct and unsmoothed. Stick input uses the existing dead zone, then a radial response exponent. Camera settings persist independent sensitivity multipliers (0.25–3, default 1) and stick exponent (1–3, default 2). Focus, menu and diagnostic suppression retain their existing owners.

Local chase-distance, inertia and aerial-pullback preferences use the same camera for armed and unarmed play. Sustained flight widens the view and stabilizes the launch heading/pivot; landing blends back to chase. The final rendered center ray still supplies desired aim from the authoritative weapon pivot, including parallax. Aerial framing never follows accepted mount rotation or changes weapon authority, limits, input sensitivity, or network messages.

Only living, visible rival cars qualify. Mouse assistance requires held RMB; existing remappable controller camera input can initiate controller assistance without a physical mouse button. Fire, equip and driving input never initiate assistance. Machine Gun can acquire near the centered cursor, choosing the smallest angular distance among eligible cars with native centre-line visibility. Acquisition uses half the shared retention cone, plus the bounded close-car extent; the default angular margins are 2.5 degrees for acquisition and 5 degrees for retention, each plus at most 0.1 radian of extent. The smaller acquisition region avoids requiring precise initial intersection while the larger retention region tolerates small misses. Retention requires an onscreen centre, native centre-line visibility and the same living capability. Nearby rivals cannot steal a still-eligible target; an intervening car or world cover releases it. Missiles, mines, rocks, pickups and every other non-vehicle entity are excluded by the existing car roster; their collision/occlusion behavior is unchanged.

Initial Machine Gun engagement attracts the camera toward the body centre at exponential rate 8/s, capped by shared per-device tuning (18 degrees/s mouse, 24 degrees/s controller). Relative world-bearing motion from rival movement and lens translation is compensated separately, bounded to 180 degrees/s with shortest-path yaw wrapping. Grounded vehicle heading changes are cancelled in the engaged orbit offset, so steering is not an instruction to look away. Active engagement holds ordinary recentering. RMB release immediately clears assistance and heading compensation; the existing smooth chase return follows the current car. Deliberate breakaway while aim intent remains active fades heading compensation at 8/s. Obstruction, pitch limits and lifecycle resets remain authoritative for camera presentation; driving controls and physics do not change.

Existing input gains/inversion, stick curve and friction still apply. Small approach input outside the projected body frame retains initial attraction. Deliberate fine input on the body frame ends centre attraction for that engagement while movement compensation continues, preserving the chosen angular offset instead of pulling placement back to centre. Strong aim-away (over 180 mouse pixels/s or 0.65 stick magnitude), including a sweep past centre, releases promptly. Slow outward input also releases after the cursor leaves the projected body frame with a four-pixel release tolerance; that tolerance does not enlarge the rendered brackets or hitbox. A dismissed car cannot immediately reacquire when the player stops beside it: moving back toward it or leaving its acquisition region rearms it. A 0.18-second delay and continuing-strong-input guard prevent repeated grabs during a sweep. Release uses inversion-adjusted input before sensitivity gains so low sensitivity cannot trap deliberate input. Offscreen/covered/ineligible targets, capability/life changes, suppression, accepted-aim expiry and reconstruction clear retention. Button transitions carry a local input-intent revision, so even release/repress between rendered frames clears the old target, dismissal and placement state. Focus/menu/diagnostic suppression and loss of gameplay camera ownership clear the input intent; focus restoration alone cannot restore engagement. Assistance-only reset leaves ordinary cursor-directed desired aim and authoritative acceptance/firing intact. No target identity, dismissal or offset is replicated or restored. Standard Missile retains its existing near-axis friction without this engagement behavior or firing integration.

The logical cursor stays exactly at the viewport center through rotation, tilt, recentering and resizing. White means accepted readiness, orange means deployment/self-clearance restriction. Four disconnected red/white corners surround the acquired/retained car; they have no connecting edges or fill and identify the same car receiving assistance. The production body envelope projects from local x ±1.5, y -0.7..1.3, z ±2.6 metres, clipped to the viewport, without extra pixel padding. During engagement, desired aim follows the actual centered camera ray at the car's body-centre depth (displayed local centre `(0, 0.3, 0)`), converted from the authoritative rack pivot. This retains lens/pivot parallax while permitting off-centre placement; the frame does not force centre-directed shots. Without a framed car, the ray supplies ordinary free aim, bounded by native cover. Both paths require the same accepted articulation/readiness/clearance before firing. The HUD never claims perfect shot alignment. The 200 m grouping benchmark applies to settled centre aim, not deliberate off-centre placement.

After camera follow, the same center ray resolves native geometry for HUD intersection and conversion to direction from the authoritative pivot, accounting for close-range parallax. World cover bounds the ray first; per-car queries transform that segment into each native chassis hull's collision pose, then transform the closest result back into its displayed pose. This follows remote interpolation and local correction smoothing without moving gameplay colliders. The HUD does not project the accepted weapon ray or claim perfect shot alignment. Mount presentation eases toward accepted angles at 30/s; smoothing has no firing authority.

## Replication and tuning

All [Camera preferences](settings.md) apply on the shared rendered camera before the centered ray is resolved: FOV, height, distance, inertia, aerial pullback, shake, axis/controller gains, inversion and recentering. The ray's current origin and direction supply the viewed point; conversion from the authoritative rack pivot still handles parallax. FOV is never replaced with a fixed projection in the aim path. No camera preference enters host tuning or messages; only resulting desired aim uses the established protocol. Shake displaces the lens, so it participates in the same rendered ray/HUD solution instead of using an inconsistent unshaken ray. Existing rate, pitch and self-clearance limits may restrict accepted articulation independently of intent, as before.

Item protocol version 21 carries replaceable aim kinds on the existing item path. Requests are exactly 56 bytes; publications carry generation, configuration revision, tick and at most eight solutions (29–557 bytes). Subtype bounds precede allocation. Existing connection/epoch fencing and host/generation/configuration/tick validation remain mandatory.

Requests/publications run at 20 Hz; authority advances at 60 Hz. Aim does not advance reliable item outcome revisions or resend complete world state. Empty sessions produce no continuing aim traffic. Retirement samples clear presentation, with a 300 ms local expiry covering lost samples/outages. Recovery starts without stale aim.

Eight host-validated **Configs → Weapon aiming** controls expose turn rate, pitch limits, retention cone, per-device friction and per-device attraction through the existing catalog. Configuration version 45 carries them through live replication and recovery. Local sensitivities stay outside synchronization. Host settings schema 3 migrates the previous persisted six-degree default spread once; other custom values and deliberate schema-3 overrides are preserved.

## Verification

Core tests cover directional reach, rate/clearance, ownership, ordering, expiry, restore and codec bounds. Pure driver tests cover sender/configuration/generation/tick guards and outage expiry. Camera tests exercise mouse/stick response at 30/60/144 Hz.

The native three-peer aiming harness first exercises actual Machine Gun bursts forward, sideways, rearward, at an elevated airborne target and beside an adjacent car. It checks native damage, cone/origin correctness, full deployed scale, fixed rack, observer yaw/pitch and ordered remote shot endpoints. It also exercises two simultaneous sustained shooters, self-blocked fire, release, aim expiry/recovery, suppression and slot switching, then restores the existing shared-camera scenarios. Synthetic target trajectories isolate firing from driving accuracy.

The native three-peer aiming harness also sweeps every Camera control independently at low/default/high values (both Invert Y states), in grounded/airborne upward/downward views: 152 cases. It compares an independently queried final center ray with pivot-relative local intent, checks constrained authoritative articulation, observer mount presentation, body clearance, centered HUD and unchanged host tuning/peer FOV. These are synthetic native inputs and real same-machine UDP; they do not establish physical-controller ergonomics or multi-device Internet behavior.

`check-weapon-aim.ps1 -GodotPath <exe> [-Visual] [-Impaired] [-Oval]` composes three real UDP peers, production Cars, native queries, input and Combat HUD. Default geometry is an isolated prototype platform; `-Oval` uses the production map. Impairment adds 30 ms outbound delay, 5 ms jitter and 2% loss. Scenarios include centered mouse/stick/recentering/resize presentation, near-car acquisition versus free aim outside the acquisition region and intervening cover, direction changes, elevated/fast-crossing targets, side-by-side proximity, driving, suppression, switching, NOS then high-speed direct-fire selection, removal and new life. Target trajectories are fixture-authored; shooter movement/NOS use native input/simulation. Captures/logs go under `.godot/aim-checks`. The fixture isolates scripted input from desktop events and restores synthetic held input after desktop focus changes; it does not verify physical input ergonomics or operating-system focus behavior. Captures occur outside the timed freedom-driving sample to avoid PNG encoding stalls interrupting held-use delivery.

`-Accuracy` measures six 2,400-round authoritative samples at 200 m against the actual projected frame (yaw 0/45/90 degrees, FOV 50/90), counting every ray and native collision separately. It uses an enlarged fixture magazine at unchanged cadence and held airborne fixture poses to remove map obstruction. `-Sticky` isolates acquired-car movement, disabled-pull comparison, mouse/controller breakaway, actual unframed fire, crossing/cover and non-vehicle presentation exclusion. `-Freedom` starts with actual Godot RMB/LMB press/hold/release events through adapter capture: initially released free fire, stationary held acquisition, release while LMB stays held, rapid taps/repress, focus notifications and controller activation without RMB. It then checks near-car acquisition, maintained off-centre placement with actual shots, deliberate switching, gradual release/dismissal/rearming, and assisted versus unassisted native steering/throttle while firing at a fixture-driven moving rival. It also checks return to normal chase yaw and retention through fixture-launched native ballistic flight and landing. Full mode includes the freedom scenarios. These modes reuse the same production aiming and firing paths.

The fixture also exercises a real missile-attributed death and ordinary timed respawn after active aiming. Nitro must remain selected to boost; the existing two-slot system cannot simultaneously select a direct-fire weapon. Scripted events update Godot input state and enter the production input adapter explicitly; automatic desktop-event dispatch is disabled in this fixture so physical mouse movement cannot contaminate a rendered run. Synthetic stick input does not establish physical-controller ergonomics, and same-machine UDP does not establish multi-device Internet behavior. See the [current verification report](../verification/ts-262.md) for observed results and limitations.

Native response probes compare attraction with disabled pull, exercise moving-car retention and deliberate mouse/controller breakaway, then check crossing rivals, intervening cover, non-vehicle presentation and free fire. A bounded UDP packet blackout checks accepted-aim expiry and fresh-sample recovery; it is distinct from authenticated reconnection. Close-target diagnostics record displayed/native target geometry and fixed-point drift so a fixture miss is not silently treated as a HUD failure or a pass.
