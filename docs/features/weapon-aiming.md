# Camera-relative weapon aiming

The assisted indicator exposes its current projected top edge, including glow, to the [remote name/HP layout](hud.md#remote-vehicle-tags). The arena updates that stack after camera and marker presentation so it clears the chevron at the current distance, FOV and resolution. Marker dimensions retain no targeting or accuracy meaning.

The shared direct-fire foundation serves Machine Gun, standard Missile and the EMP Claw launcher. EMP uses a 0.37 m conservative clearance radius for its enlarged cannon; the existing weapons retain their zero-radius aiming test. Salvo keeps its separate mechanics. The production Car's deployed rack stays fixed: its child mount yaws and the child payload pitches. Missile uses its production Blender turret and telescoping payload; Machine Gun uses the Blender-authored Twin Fang with co-rotating suspended drums and flexible ammunition feeds.

Machine Gun and standard Missile consume the ready accepted direction and production rack pivot for authoritative fire. Machine Gun adds deterministic spread calibrated for approximately 80% grouping inside a production car's frame at 200 m. Missile launches one round along the accepted direction without spread; its later terrain correction never consumes an aim target or vehicle position. Missile driving fire without camera-aim intent requests chassis-forward direction, while held mouse aim or the existing controller camera intent uses shared free aim and car assistance. The centered cursor represents camera intent, not a guarantee of impact or an accepted-ray reticle.

## Authority and lifecycle

`Core.Items.WeaponAim` solves chassis-relative yaw/pitch from a finite unit direction and authoritative vehicle pose. `ItemAuthority` owns transient intent and accepted solutions; `HostVehicleSession` enforces sender, generation and participation. Requests bind to current life, selected grant token, selection watermark and monotonic counter, never a claimed player or hit outcome.

The accepted contract contains owner/life/token/tick, yaw, pitch, origin/direction, clearance and readiness. Full yaw supports side/rear aim. Host tuning bounds angular rate and pitch; upward travel defaults to and supports 90 degrees. At the vertical pole the mount preserves its previous yaw. A tick advances articulation at most once, including retries of rejected gameplay batches. A conservative production-body ray test covers the pivot-to-exit path, so offsetting a muzzle cannot skip the owner. Self-intersecting requests clamp to a clear pose but remain not ready. The shared pivot is the deployed rack payload attachment at local `(0, 1.81, 1.845)` metres.

Readiness starts on accepted ownership/selection, independently of first camera input. A shared two-phase mechanical helper serves the existing Client rack animation and a conservative Core readiness gate. Missile additionally requires its captured inventory readiness deadline; local use waits for its full rigid deployment. The generic gate integrates current trunk/rack speeds every tick, reserving initial deployment or full retraction/deployment on a capability replacement, even without a slot switch. Replacing Nitro additionally reserves its 0.24-second nozzle nesting. Capability history survives empty-slot and aim-only resets; life change, departure and recovery clear it. Client animation retains its existing smooth partial-cycle reversal rather than snapping to a conservative phase. Selection, depletion, invalid life, death, removal and suspension retire transient aim. Requests expire after 15 host ticks. No additional inventory, damage or checkpoint authority is introduced.

Aim preparation uses the exact current native observation batch passed to the item step, matching the existing weapon handlers' pre-command pose phase. If that item/world transaction throws, the host restores transient aim and readiness state before retry. Presentation remains downstream of a successful host step.

An accepted Proxy Mine placement retains ownership of the existing rack until placement and its 0.23-second arm return finish. Selecting or acquiring a direct-fire capability during that placement holds its readiness gate before the replacement path; it does not interrupt or redesign mine placement.

## Camera, assistance and HUD

Selected direct-fire items share the close, low [chase camera](camera.md), orbiting the fully extended rack attachment used by `WeaponAim.Pivot`. The elevated boom and shallow viewing angle leave the payload below the centered cursor. The deployed reference remains stable while stowed/deploying; accepted weapon rotation never feeds back into camera orientation. The shared camera can tilt 90 degrees upward and 85 degrees downward, equally with and without a weapon. Upward view pitch retains the chase lens position instead of lowering the camera around the chassis; downward input retains its elevated orbit. RMB mouse movement and the remapped camera stick change desired aim. Releasing RMB always ends mouse assistance and resumes the same smooth chase return. Neutral controller input retains an eligible engagement initiated by the existing camera controls; otherwise it returns normally. Equipping a weapon introduces no separate camera pose. The shared weapon contract still supports its independently bounded chassis-relative yaw/pitch; camera controls remain constrained by the camera's view limits.

Mouse displacement remains direct and unsmoothed. Stick input uses the existing dead zone, then a radial response exponent. Camera settings persist independent sensitivity multipliers (0.25–3, default 1) and stick exponent (1–3, default 2). Focus, menu and diagnostic suppression retain their existing owners.

Local chase-distance, inertia and aerial-pullback preferences use the same camera for armed and unarmed play. Sustained flight widens the view and stabilizes the launch heading/pivot; landing blends back to chase. The final rendered center ray still supplies desired aim from the authoritative weapon pivot, including parallax. Aerial framing never follows accepted mount rotation or changes weapon authority, limits, input sensitivity, or network messages.

Only living, visible rival cars qualify. Mouse assistance requires held RMB; existing remappable controller camera input can initiate controller assistance without a physical mouse button. Fire, equip and driving input never initiate assistance. Acquisition measures angular distance to the nearest point of the existing projected body envelope, so a close broadside body does not require precise centre alignment. The margin is half the configured cone for large/intermediate bodies (2.5 degrees by default), increasing continuously toward the full cone for a small apparent body (at most 5 degrees). Apparent radius is half the projected diagonal's angle; the extra distant margin retires by half a cone of radius. Candidate ranking prefers the nearest envelope, with centre angle breaking ties. This changes only selection, not marker dimensions, hitboxes or shot grouping. Acquisition can occur during continuous mouse/stick approach: a strong gesture rejects only candidates behind the direction of departure, rather than suspending every acquisition until input stops. Retention requires an onscreen centre, native body visibility and the same living capability. Visibility first checks the centre, then up to five inset body samples if world geometry blocks that ray. Each sample must hit the actual displayed native hull through an unobstructed closest-hit ray; hidden samples never qualify. This preserves a partially visible car beside a narrow obstacle or crest without retaining a fully hidden car. An intervening car on the centre ray still releases immediately. Nearby rivals cannot steal a still-eligible target. Missiles, mines, rocks, pickups and every other non-vehicle entity are excluded by the existing car roster; their collision/occlusion behavior is unchanged.

Acquired-target framing is separate from acquisition: angular distance from the body may temporarily exceed the configured retention cone for up to 0.45 seconds, within three cones capped at 20 degrees. Framing rejection runs after camera follow/compensation, once per render. Returning inside the ordinary retention cone clears the recovery timer. RMB release, eligibility/life/capability loss and accepted-aim expiry are checked before follow. Spatial visibility uses the current final collision-safe lens, avoiding comparisons between a stale camera and newly presented bodies. Off-screen centre and native cover release before tracking correction, and final framing is checked afterward; recovery never bypasses these safeguards. Optional local release diagnostics identify angular recovery exhaustion separately from occlusion, input/lifecycle reset and deliberate departure. No diagnostic or recovery state is replicated.

Initial direct-fire engagement attracts the camera toward the body centre at exponential rate 8/s, capped by shared per-device tuning (18 degrees/s mouse, 24 degrees/s controller). Relative world-bearing motion from rival movement and lens translation is compensated separately, bounded to 180 degrees/s with shortest-path yaw wrapping. Unapplied motion remains pending after a jolt instead of being discarded by that cap. Tracking evaluates the current lens after boom travel, shake and obstruction, then rotates within the existing pitch limits without moving the collision-safe lens. This removes the previous positional tracking delay. Grounded vehicle heading changes are cancelled in the engaged orbit offset, so steering is not an instruction to look away. Active engagement holds ordinary recentering. RMB release immediately clears assistance and heading compensation; the existing smooth chase return follows the current car. Deliberate breakaway while aim intent remains active fades heading compensation at 8/s. Obstruction, pitch limits and lifecycle resets remain authoritative for camera presentation; driving controls and physics do not change.

Existing input gains/inversion, stick curve and friction still apply. Small approach input outside the projected body frame retains initial attraction. Deliberate fine input on the body frame or within 30% of the assistance cone establishes a chosen camera angular offset, so a distant car does not require outline-perfect correction. The exact sensitivity/friction/curve-adjusted look displacement updates that offset, and the existing bounded correction recovers around it after involuntary motion instead of returning it to centre. It uses inversion-adjusted physical input before sensitivity gains, with quadratic stick weighting so small stick corrections remain fine input. Low sensitivity cannot trap a deliberate sweep. A continuing sweep cannot rearm the same car merely by circling past it. A 0.18-second delay, dismissed-car memory and candidate-relative continuing-gesture guard prevent repeated grabs during departure while allowing approach to another car. Offscreen/covered/ineligible targets, capability/life changes, suppression, accepted-aim expiry and reconstruction clear retention. The ray's current origin and direction supply the viewed point; conversion from the authoritative rack pivot still handles parallax. FOV is never replaced with a fixed projection in the aim path. Shake displaces the lens, so it participates in the same rendered ray/HUD solution instead of using an inconsistent unshaken ray. Existing rate, pitch and self-clearance limits may restrict accepted articulation independently of intent, as before.

Item protocol version 23 carries replaceable aim kinds on the existing item path. Requests are exactly 56 bytes; publications carry generation, configuration revision, tick and at most eight solutions (29–557 bytes). Subtype bounds precede allocation. Existing connection/epoch fencing and host/generation/configuration/tick validation remain mandatory.

Requests/publications run at 20 Hz; authority advances at 60 Hz. Aim does not advance reliable item outcome revisions or resend complete world state. Empty sessions produce no continuing aim traffic. Retirement samples clear presentation, with a 300 ms local expiry covering lost samples/outages. Recovery starts without stale aim.

Eight host-validated **Configs → Weapon aiming** controls expose turn rate, pitch limits, retention cone, per-device friction and per-device attraction through the existing catalog. Configuration version 46 carries them through live replication and recovery. Local sensitivities stay outside synchronization. Host settings schema 3 migrates the previous persisted six-degree default spread once; other custom values and deliberate schema-3 overrides are preserved.
