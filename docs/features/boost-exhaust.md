# Boost exhaust presentation

`BoostExhaust` is a reconstructable Client component shared by practice `VehicleBody` and `NetworkVehicleBody`, parented to each production WeaponRack. `CarRackPresentation` readies it from confirmed selected Nitro ownership. It reads the current vehicle snapshot for ignition: only an interacting vehicle with active Nitro starts sustained combustion. Ending combustion has a bounded visual tail; inactive overspeed recovery cannot start or prolong it. It does not consume input or author propulsion. Core owns a replicated 36-tick deployment timer: thrust and fuel consumption wait for full deployment, while Client reconstructs the mechanical pose from that timer.

The local [chase camera](camera.md#boost-speed-sensation) reads that same active movement state for smooth FOV expansion, bounded pull-back and peripheral speed lines. Camera activation follows accepted thrust immediately, after deployment; the exhaust retains its mechanical deployment and priming sequence. Neither presentation component controls the other's lifetime or changes propulsion.

## Hardware and lifecycle

The [Blender-authored rear jet](../../assets/vehicles/boost/README.md) references the actual production Car master for fit. The armored chamber hangs beneath the trunk weapon rack on four braces and diagonal stays. A forward-facing red intake above the rack connects through a swept duct; fuel lines, casing bands, heat shielding and a recessed turbine/hub fill out the approved mechanical design. The turbine rotates independently of the fixed outlet stators, idling at 2 radians/s and smoothly accelerating toward 10 radians/s during use. Selecting Nitro opens the deck, raises the assembly with the rack (revealed once the underslung chamber clears the bay floor), then extends a nested sleeve .14 m and the inner barrel/nozzle .28 m rearward with hydraulic rods. The original Car and rack geometry remain unchanged. Nitro uses the persistent jet as its rack payload; switching away nests the barrel before lowering and replacing the payload.

Selected Nitro deployment takes .60 seconds in total: .12 seconds to nest/lower any previous payload, .32 seconds to open the deck and raise the rack, and .16 seconds to extend the nozzle. These fixed phases preserve the existing geometry, ordered mechanical path and smooth easing. They override configurable cosmetic rack speeds for Nitro, so tuning cannot delay readiness beyond the gameplay deadline. Accepted active Boost after full extension begins a 0.30-second sparks-and-fumes phase, then opens a 40 ms flame ignition envelope with a brief size/brightness overshoot. Three instances of the authored UV shell form a hot core, primary flame and outer wisps. `BoostFlame.gdshader` uses additive unshaded rendering in the production Compatibility renderer. Travelling noise tears, longitudinal pulses, shock-cell modulation and tip displacement keep sustained thrust moving. Observed speed stretches the plume; stable vehicle/layer phases avoid synchronized fleet pulses. The throat heats and cools independently. No screen texture, dynamic light, bloom or renderer change is required.

Normal release smoothly shrinks and fades the current flame over .45 seconds, with diminishing smoke emission and cooling throat. Confirmed depletion instead produces a .60-second terminal sequence: a broader, brighter orange pulse, intensified sparks and smoke, then a flickering collapse. CarRackPresentation identifies depletion when the same selected Nitro capability becomes empty in a confirmed inventory publication; missing inventory, selection changes and prediction expiry cannot originate the burst. It occurs once per observed capability disposal and is never replayed from a recovery baseline. Gameplay thrust still stops immediately. Existing world-space smoke fades over at most 1.5 seconds; sparks last .28 seconds. The selected nozzle remains extended and ready after release; selecting alone never ignites it. The nozzle stays extended until combustion finishes, then switching/depletion can nest it over .24 seconds before the rack lowers. A held use during deployment engages the selected capability but produces no thrust and consumes no fuel until its remaining deployment ticks reach zero. Release cancels that pending activation; selection alone can finish deployment without igniting. The .60-second deployment, existing .30-second prime and .04-second ignition envelope complete within one second at normal frame rates. Repeated use while ready does not cycle the hardware, but each activation restarts priming. Release during priming clears pending ignition without creating a new flame. A rapid re-press allows the previous tail to decay until new ignition takes over. New life, loss of participation/visibility, large pose discontinuity and explicit network reseed immediately clear tails, historical particles and pose. Vehicle removal frees owned runtime resources; imported assets remain shared.

## Budgets and verification

Exhaust animation and shutdown consume actual nonnegative elapsed render time, matching the camera's time basis. Slow frames do not stretch the .45-second release or .60-second depletion countdown into a longer combustion effect. A frame that spans the remaining tail clears flame and new particle emission on that update; existing world-space particles still own their natural lifetime.

The rendered exhaust fixture checks a 700 ms frame spanning each shutdown tail. The integrated `check-boost-camera.ps1` fixture additionally drives three simultaneous boosters on the production oval, cancels sub-priming taps, re-engages during release, and checks settled exhaust and camera state together. `check-nitro.ps1` keeps both production peer chase cameras current and verifies their sustained response and recovery alongside local/remote exhaust and confirmed depletion. Neither fixture claims Internet or separate-device multiplayer coverage.

Each vehicle has three shared-mesh flame instances (1,728 triangles each), one 192-particle smoke emitter and one 24-particle spark emitter. Priming uses 55% smoke emission and full sparks; sustained thrust uses full smoke and 25% sparks. Depletion temporarily uses the existing full spark/smoke budgets without allocating another emitter. Hardware is batched by material under each moving root. No per-activation nodes are allocated. Smoke emission ends beyond 65 m from the active camera, sparks beyond 35 m, and flames beyond 120 m. Particle/flame shadows are disabled. These are presentation budgets, not gameplay settings. The particle material applies vertex color for lifetime alpha and particle billboarding for correct size/rotation.

`check-boost-exhaust.ps1 -GodotPath <exe>` renders the production vehicle/map/environment through idle, selection-only rack rise/extension, ready without ignition, rotating fan, front intake/engine detail views, sparks/fumes before ignition, cancelled short priming, ignition, sustained motion, release while ready, switching/stow, rapid reuse, 1/8/32-car views, reseed, new life and death. Captures are written under `.godot/ts225-vfx`; `--boost-loop` repeats its scene for observation and Space restarts the sequence. Scripted poses isolate presentation, not physical driving or 32 network connections. `check-nitro.ps1` supplies actual two-peer native driving, held/released/reused/depleted Nitro and local/remote presentation cutoff checks. `check-car-rack.ps1` verifies Nitro as a selected rack payload and other inventory transitions. Visual observation remains necessary to judge motion and readability.

See [vehicle networking](vehicle-networking.md), [vehicles](vehicles.md) and [Nitro authority](items.md#sustained-nitro-resource) for unchanged source-state contracts.

The rendered fixture also checks monotonic release decay, re-press during the tail, a distinct depletion peak, delayed stow, and death interrupting a terminal burst. Its optional --boost-clips argument saves sampled animation frames (use Godot --fixed-fps 60 to isolate readback overhead from sequence timing). Native Nitro checks observe release tails and depletion bursts for both cars on both peer views, then assert bounded cutoff. These visual tails do not alter score, charge or rocket-force assertions.
