# Player Vehicle Chase Camera

## Behavior and Orientation

Selected direct-fire weapons use [weapon aiming](weapon-aiming.md) with the same close, low chase view as unarmed driving. The orbit pivot is the shared fully deployed weapon attachment, local `(0, 1.81, 1.845)` metres, transformed by the displayed chassis pose. It stays at that reference location while stowed or deploying, avoiding an equip/retract camera jump. The authored 6.4 m boom uses a default/minimum 1.15 distance multiplier, placing the lens 7.36 m behind and 1.25 m above this pivot, with a shallow 3.5-degree downward view. The boom and viewing angle are independent: the rack and payload sit below the central aiming area instead of covering the cursor. Horizontal input rotates the boom around the rack. Downward look raises it on the existing orbit; upward look tilts only the view, retaining the chase lens height and distance. The camera drives desired weapon aim and never reads back articulated weapon rotation, avoiding a feedback loop.

Releasing RMB or returning the camera stick to neutral restores this chase view, including with a weapon selected. Per-device local aiming sensitivity/curve and bounded input-only near-target friction apply during deliberate input. The camera supplies desired intent; host-accepted aim and clearance remain Core-owned.

Practice and network gameplay share Client.Vehicles.VehicleChaseCamera. In ordinary grounded driving, camera yaw follows the already-interpolated/displayed vehicle heading directly on every update, with a level horizon and a fixed downward viewing angle. Invalid, near-vertical or overturned orientations retain the last usable heading. Sustained flight and its landing recovery use the stable heading and bounded catch-up described below. Positional inertia provides motion and weight. New vehicle identities and life generations reset follow and feedback memory immediately.

Holding RMB enables mouse orbit around the local displayed vehicle; releasing it smoothly returns behind the vehicle. The existing remappable right-stick camera intent also orbits, and neutral stick returns automatically. RMB holds the current offset even without movement; mouse and stick motion can combine. Steering alone cannot change the free-look offset. With no free-look offset, displayed heading determines chase yaw immediately during ordinary grounded driving.

Mouse sensitivity is 0.003 radians per screen-relative pixel, independent of render duration and viewport stretching. Full stick speed is 2.2 radians/second, with diagonal magnitude limited to one. Camera analog bindings use at least a 0.15 dead zone (or the larger configured input dead zone), so reducing driving dead zone cannot disable camera noise rejection. Yaw wraps around the vehicle; the viewing pitch reaches 85 degrees upward and 85 degrees downward. These limits apply equally with and without a weapon. Exponential recentering at 6/s follows the current displayed heading while returning. Upward input above the neutral chase angle leaves the positional boom at that angle, including the Boost boom offset, so aiming upward cannot lower the camera toward the ground. Downward input retains the elevated orbit. The chase distance/height define that orbit radius around the deployed rack attachment; inertia remains in the vehicle-heading frame and does not redirect aim.

`PlayerInput` retains sole mouse-capture ownership and collects RMB-gated screen motion. Its existing focus, menu, diagnostic and arena gates suppress camera input and discard queued mouse motion. One render follow consumes the accumulated motion exactly once. Vehicle identity/life changes discard orbit and queued motion; network resynchronization explicitly resets the same camera even when identity/life remain unchanged. A reset first presents the chase baseline; subsequent fresh input can orbit again. Arena reconstruction starts a fresh camera.

## Positional Inertia and Feedback

Measured world velocity is sampled once per newer vehicle movement snapshot. Velocity differences use elapsed physics ticks, rather than render duration, to estimate acceleration. Render-time exponential damping blends bounded offsets in the heading-relative frame: forward acceleration moves the camera slightly rearward, braking/deceleration moves it forward, and lateral acceleration moves it toward the outside of a turn. Actual sideways velocity adds restrained lateral weight during sliding/drift. Constant straight velocity removes the acceleration response; neutral sideways velocity removes the slip response. No raw steering, drift button or vehicle command drives these effects.

Horizontal follow uses the displayed position plus the chase offset and bounded inertia. Vertical anchor damping absorbs bumps. Default fore/aft offset is limited to 0.2 m and lateral offset to 0.12 m, converging at 20/s. Vertical lag is capped at 0.18 m on the ground and 0.06 m in sustained flight. There is no unbounded world-space horizontal lag. Boost adds the bounded presentation response below.

Local contacts above the configurable severity threshold produce a short two-axis shake in the camera's view plane. Severity uses normal contact velocity or impulse divided by mass. A square-root response curve makes useful intermediate impacts readable at chase distance while retaining a silent threshold and a bounded maximum. Accepted damage outcomes use the square root of actual HP lost divided by 50 (clamped to one), deduplicated by sequence within the vehicle life. Network contacts already presented at a prediction tick are not replayed by reconciliation. Impulses coalesce using the strongest envelope, with a short contact cooldown and exponential decay. Horizontal/vertical oscillations use differing frequencies, normalized to remain inside the envelope; they settle without rotating the horizon, changing aim or changing chase depth. Feedback cannot change vehicle forces or HP.

## Aerial framing and local preferences

Distance remains at chase framing for the first 0.35 seconds without support. A smoothstep curve then increases the requested pullback over one second of additional airtime, reaching a 3.6 m target at default strength after 1.35 seconds. Distance converges outward at 4/s, so short jumps receive only a small response while sustained flight gains the full view. Landing immediately stops further expansion and eases distance inward at 6/s. The local airtime counter samples newer movement ticks, so repeated rendering cannot manufacture flight and unsupported crash tumbles remain eligible even when gameplay air control is disabled. The existing air-control timer seeds observed airtime; no new simulation state or network field is added.

Orientation stabilization starts earlier, after 0.12 seconds without support: the camera retains its launch heading and blends the rotating rack reference toward its level, heading-relative location at 5/s. This keeps flips and rolls readable without forcing a full-distance response to small hops. Mouse/stick orbit remains available around that stable view. Landing blends toward the usable displayed heading along the shortest arc with a 4.5 rad/s angular-speed ceiling. Any pullback already earned in flight and the stable pivot remain until the heading converges, without requesting additional distance on the ground; normal heading follow then resumes and pivot stabilization retires at 6/s. All distance changes precede the unchanged world-obstruction solver. Safety may constrain the requested distance.

**Settings → Camera** offers **Chase distance** (1.15–1.5×, default 1.15), **Camera inertia** (0–1, default 0.5), and **Aerial pullback** (0–1.5×, default 1). Distance scales the ground boom from 7.36–9.6 m and blends live edits at 8/s. Older saved values below 1.15 clamp to the new minimum. Inertia scales the bounded offsets and vertical lag from zero to twice their tight defaults; horizontal translation never gains world-space lag. Aerial strength permits zero extra distance through 5.4 m. Stable aerial orientation remains active even at zero pullback. Preferences apply to both practice and network cameras through the existing local settings provider, persist on the device, and never enter host Configs. Identity/life/reseed boundaries clear aerial and follow history.

## Local camera controls

The dedicated **Settings → Camera** page also provides independent horizontal/vertical look gains (0.25–3×), controller camera sensitivity (0.25–3×), Invert Y, recenter rate (0.25–3×), base FOV (50–90°, default 65°), and vertical chase height (0.5–3 m above the deployed rack reference, default 1.25 m). The axis gains, inversion and controller gain work equally armed/unarmed. Radial armed-stick shaping precedes axis gains; inversion precedes direction-dependent friction. The existing armed mouse gain and stick curve remain available on Camera with their original keys. Recenter scales only neutral-input exponential return; held RMB or deliberate input retains its existing ownership. Active Machine Gun car engagement temporarily holds that return while bounded per-device attraction helps retain the visible car. Deliberate aim-away releases it promptly; ordinary chase return resumes outside engagement. The shared aiming owner supplies attraction through this same look path, after local input gains/inversion and before world obstruction.

Height changes the positional boom relative to the car and does not scale vertical input. FOV changes projection, including the obstruction solver's near-plane radius; speed/Boost expansion still adds to the selected base. All settings apply before the final rendered center ray supplies shared aim intent and HUD intersection. Neither accepted mount rotation nor weapon firing handlers feed back into the camera. Ground/air transitions, view limits, host articulation/clearance and network contracts retain their existing owners. Preferences remain local and use the existing settings controller/store.

## Boost speed sensation

The shared camera reads `CanInteract`, active `Movement.Nitro` and observed horizontal `VehicleSnapshot.Speed`. `BoostCameraMotion` adds up to 7 degrees of active-Boost FOV expansion plus up to 1 degree of road-speed expansion, and extends the existing boom by at most 0.55 m along its view axis. The authored FOV is captured when the camera enters the tree (65 degrees in gameplay). Heading, aim, pitch, steering and gameplay physics are unchanged. Expansion precedes the existing obstruction sweep, so the camera clearance calculation includes the wider near plane and longer boom.

Road-speed intensity uses a smoothstep over 12–60 m/s, independent of the vehicle's tunable cap, mass or thrust. Active Boost supplies 45% of its camera response even from rest, growing with observed speed. Exponential entry at 5/s gives a clear activation response without a separate kick or shake; release at 3.5/s restores the road-speed baseline. Repeated presses retarget the same bounded envelope and never stack impulses. Inactive Nitro recovery cannot sustain the active-Boost contribution. Jump height and falling velocity do not inflate road speed.

`CameraSpeedStreaks` draws 16 sparse, translucent blue-white wind wisps starting in the middle-side bands and travelling outward offscreen on canvas layer 0, beneath the existing layer-1 HUD. Each passage independently resamples side, vertical position, short/long length, travel rate and opacity from a reproducible random stream. Independent phases prevent mirrored rows or equally spaced bars; three alpha segments soften each wisp at its ends. Their travel follows measured speed; their opacity follows road speed and the Boost envelope, with only 4% baseline intensity outside Boost. Peak per-wisp alpha varies from 0.07 to 0.14. Leading tips start around 26%/74% of screen width; the longest trailing segments stay outside the central third. The central driving/targeting area remains clear. Motion away from the camera's viewing direction attenuates the lines; side/rear free-look and reverse travel suppress misleading outward flow. No blur, distortion, post-processing pass, added shake, renderer change or per-activation nodes are used. Existing [Boost exhaust](boost-exhaust.md) retains independent ignition and shutdown timing from the same movement state.

Identity/life changes, explicit camera reseed and arena reconstruction clear the speed envelope and line phase. Loss of participation clears the camera effect; only the current local camera draws lines. All state remains Client-local and adds no configuration or network payload.

## World obstruction

After chase/orbit, inertia and shake produce the intended position, Client sweeps a sphere against existing layer-1 solid bodies, excluding the followed vehicle and areas. The radius is at least 0.25 m and encloses the actual near-plane corners for the viewport, FOV and near distance. Neither world collision layers nor gameplay bodies are changed. Static terrain/track meshes, barriers, containers and moving solid props use their existing native collision; decorative meshes without collision do not block the camera.

The camera shortens its boom to the safe sweep distance with 3 cm clearance. A bounded 0.2-second extrapolation of unshaken camera intent anticipates corner entry (maximum target/end prediction 3/5 m), easing contraction at 12/s; current geometry always takes precedence over smoothing. Anticipation alone cannot force the camera closer than 4.8 m. Recovery waits 0.12 seconds and eases at 5/s, avoiding rapid outward/inward movement at edges. Only obstruction shortening is damped; an unobstructed camera retains its original follow/inertia/shake output.

When obstruction reduces the unraised safe distance below 5.5 m (or 0.1 m inside a shorter intended boom), a bounded lift of up to 1.8 m keeps the close view above the chassis. Its temporary pitch correction keeps the car framed without changing stored orbit intent. The original boom constraint still applies, so raising the lens cannot put it beyond a low wall that would hide the car. The raised path is swept again; initial pivot overlaps use native contact separation. Lift returns at 5/s when clear. This is obstruction framing only, not a change to the normal chase/heading/inertia model. Very tight spaces necessarily yield a close view and may crop part of the car.

A rolled chassis can extend into that raised view. When displayed up alignment falls below 0.65 and the ordinary resolved lens is within the lesser of 5.5 m or 85% of the intended boom from the follow pivot, the camera searches alternate horizontal boom angles at 15-degree intervals on both sides. Candidates must have at least the lesser of 6.5 m or 90% of the intended boom in world-volume clearance. Selection favors the previous safe angle and then the smallest departure from chase, keeping the lens on the clear side of a bank/fence rather than pulling it into the chassis. Angular easing is swept again; an unsafe intermediate angle gives way immediately to a safe candidate. Outward distance eases at 5/s. This fallback aims at the follow pivot with a level horizon; it does not change stored heading, orbit intent or Boost response. Wind-wisp alignment uses the final rendered view.

Once ordinary framing has more than the lesser of 7 m or 97% of the intended boom in clearance for 0.2 seconds, the fallback eases back to the original boom and retires when position/angle have converged. Reset/life boundaries discard its history. The candidate search only runs during this rollover/recovery fallback. It ranks angular preferences before querying geometry and stops at the first safe candidate, with at most 25 candidate sweeps per update. If no candidate provides useful clearance, existing world-safe contraction remains the last resort; arbitrary fully enclosed spaces are not guaranteed an external view.

Identity/life changes, explicit resume/reset and newly constructed cameras initialize obstruction distance, lift and prediction from the current scene immediately. No obstruction history enters Core, vehicle commands, prediction history, settings or network payloads. Queries are read-only and occur on the existing render follow path after the displayed vehicle pose updates. In tight spaces the safe world constraint takes priority over the final feedback displacement.

## Tuning and Architecture

**Settings → Camera → Camera shake** scales collision/damage displacement from 0–100%, defaulting to 100%. The local [Settings controller](settings.md) persists the preference and supplies it to both practice and network cameras, including reconstructed arenas. Scaling is applied after the bounded envelope; it never changes follow, heading, positional inertia, input, vehicle state or network configuration. Zero clears pending shake while preserving contact cooldown and consuming damage sequence IDs. Re-enabling starts with no queued disabled impact. View-plane displacement has a hard 0.65 m ceiling, with the severity envelope and decay producing substantially smaller actual offsets. The existing intensity setting scales this bounded feedback at the close chase radius.

The ChaseCamera node exposes Inspector properties for rack-relative follow distance/height (6.4 m / 1.25 m) and viewing angle (3.5 degrees downward), position damping (20/s), longitudinal/lateral acceleration gains (0.012 / 0.007 metres per m/s²), sideways-speed gain (0.004 metres per m/s), maximum inertia offsets, collision/damage gains (0.55 / 0.85), contact threshold (3 m/s), shake decay (7/s) and maximum view-plane shake (0.65 m). Live Remote Inspector tuning is supported; scene-authored instances can save exported values. Current arenas instantiate shared defaults.

Practice native bodies use Godot physics interpolation; the camera reads their interpolated pose. Network vehicles retain explicit pose interpolation/correction smoothing; the camera reads the visual pose after presentation updates. Automatic interpolation is disabled for the render-updated camera. Camera state remains entirely in Client, outside Core, input frames, prediction history and network payloads. Physics and vehicle input behavior are unchanged.

## Verification and Limits

Native camera checks additionally cover all axis/controller/recenter gains and both inversion states armed/unarmed at 30/60/144 FPS, active RMB hold, and nine FOV/height combinations. Settings checks exercise Camera navigation, removal from Gameplay, every new slider's bounds, inversion toggles and separate-process persistence. The driving fixture's minimum/maximum preference phases include FOV and height. The [weapon-aim harness](weapon-aiming.md#verification) sweeps all Camera preferences through actual native input, HUD, authoritative solutions and observer articulation.

`check-camera-responsiveness.ps1 -GodotPath <exe> [-Visual]` runs two production native cars through low/high speed, sharp steering reversal, handbrake sliding, bumps, a short hop, staged jump launches, front flips, barrel rolls, backflips, crash recovery, minimum/maximum preferences, isolated small jumps and repeated hops. The scripted fixture records observed support, rotation, inertia, pullback and camera position, checks lens clearance against world/chassis, and requires actual slide and inverted-pose coverage. `-Visual` saves chronological 15-frame samples under `.godot/ts-279`. Launch pose/velocity are fixture-authored; subsequent forces, aerial controls, contacts and recovery use production physics. Native camera checks cover 81 frame-rate/preference combinations and live changes; the shared weapon-aim harness also exercises an airborne shooter and landing through three UDP peers.

`check-boost-camera.ps1 -GodotPath <exe> [-Visual]` runs three native vehicle bodies on the production oval through Boost entry/sustain/release, rapid re-engagement during decay, steering with traffic, high-speed unboosted motion, an airborne launch/landing, cancelled short primes and three simultaneous boosters. Its verification-only inputs stage ordinary Nitro requests and selected rack presentation; production inventory ownership remains covered by `check-nitro.ps1`. It checks actual Boost/ground/air and multi-flame coverage, joint exhaust/camera settling, finite bounded camera output, active reseed and unchanged authoritative state. `-Visual` saves chronological 15-frame samples and a trace under `.godot/ts-227/playtest`. `-Interactive` offers a controllable native fixture: Space toggles held Boost, A/D set steering, S centers steering, R resets the run and Escape exits. Throttle is automatic; this fixture intentionally has no resource depletion, so real inventory/depletion checks remain with the two-peer Nitro harness. Pure tests cover 30/60/144 Hz transitions, repeated-use bounds, stationary Boost and reset. Scripted driving and sampled images do not establish prolonged human comfort or physical-controller ergonomics.

`check-camera-shake.ps1 -GodotPath <exe>` exercises actual native wall impacts, minor contacts and applied damage with a zero-shake reference camera. It measures actual projected displacement normalized to a 720p viewport, checks meaningful feedback at half/full intensity, zero suppression, settling, repeated damage, and disable/re-enable. `-Visual` records an 18-second native movie for chronological motion inspection rather than relying on a positive internal envelope value. The fast-check router includes this focused harness.

Pure Client tests cover orbit/recenter consistency at 30/60/144 FPS, pitch/yaw bounds, mouse hold/reset behavior, acceleration/braking, lateral inertia, settling and feedback. `check-camera.ps1 -GodotPath <exe>` runs native synthetic mouse/stick events through the production input owner and camera: RMB orbit/hold/release, noise rejection, gameplay-frame isolation, suppression, identity/life/reseed reset, immediate heading follow, inertia, feedback and unstable-orientation recovery. Armed/unarmed mouse and stick reach both 85-degree pitch limits; upward ground/air/Boost recovery is compared with a no-input camera to verify unchanged lens position. The fast-check router includes this harness. Native vehicle and network-vehicle checks additionally exercise mouse/controller orbit and smooth return on the actual displayed-pose path, saving phase screenshots when rendered. Practice checks include native life reset; reconnect checks hold an orbit across production resume on a reused vehicle.

`check-camera-obstruction.ps1 -GodotPath <exe>` exercises native walls, thin barriers, a building corner, a moving rigid prop, sloped geometry, actual prototype-map assets and the oval's concave bank. It checks camera volume/near-plane clearance, corner contraction, stationary stability, recovery and identity/life/resume/reconstruction resets. `-Fps 30`, `60` or `144` selects the render rate. `-Drive` exercises actual practice reverse/forward input toward a wall, orbit/recenter, driving away and a real vehicle life reset. `-Visual` records chronological native movie evidence. The fast-check router runs the focused geometry harness and identifies the driving playtest.

The geometry fixture also checks a rolled chassis beside a fence, a blocked alternate side, stationary settling, recovery and reset. Its driving recenter assertion compares with an independently followed no-input chase camera: an overturned chassis's Euler yaw is not the retained usable chase heading. The Boost oval fixture reproduces the high-speed bank/fence rollover and verifies final lens clearance against both world solids and the followed chassis, while recording framing coverage, minimum distance and camera-follow CPU timing separately from rendering cost. Chronological visual inspection remains required for readability and transition quality.

Synthetic inputs and sampled recordings do not establish prolonged human driving comfort. Safety can require immediate contraction for abrupt teleports or newly arriving geometry; smooth recovery must not ease through a solid. No camera-only world geometry is invented for non-colliding art. Spectator, replay, interior and replicated cameras are not implemented.
