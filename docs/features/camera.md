# Player Vehicle Chase Camera

## Behavior and Orientation

Practice and network gameplay share Client.Vehicles.VehicleChaseCamera. In normal chase view, camera yaw follows the already-interpolated/displayed vehicle heading directly on every update, with a level horizon and a fixed downward viewing angle. There is no additional heading smoothing or independent yaw catch-up period. Invalid, near-vertical or overturned orientations retain the last usable heading; a usable displayed heading is adopted immediately on recovery. Positional inertia provides motion and weight. New vehicle identities and life generations reset follow and feedback memory immediately.

Holding RMB enables mouse orbit around the local displayed vehicle; releasing it smoothly returns behind the vehicle. The existing remappable right-stick camera intent also orbits, and neutral stick returns automatically. RMB holds the current offset even without movement; mouse and stick motion can combine. Steering alone cannot rotate the camera. With no free-look offset, displayed heading determines chase yaw immediately, preserving the existing heading follow and positional inertia.

Mouse sensitivity is 0.003 radians per screen-relative pixel, independent of render duration and viewport stretching. Full stick speed is 2.2 radians/second, with diagonal magnitude limited to one. Camera analog bindings use at least a 0.15 dead zone (or the larger configured input dead zone), so reducing driving dead zone cannot disable camera noise rejection. Yaw wraps around the vehicle; pitch stays between 5 and 75 degrees downward to preserve a level horizon and avoid flipping through the poles. Exponential recentering at 6/s follows the current displayed heading while returning. The chase distance/height define the orbit radius around the existing 0.5 m aim height; inertia remains in the vehicle-heading frame and does not redirect aim.

`PlayerInput` retains sole mouse-capture ownership and collects RMB-gated screen motion. Its existing focus, menu, diagnostic and arena gates suppress camera input and discard queued mouse motion. One render follow consumes the accumulated motion exactly once. Vehicle identity/life changes discard orbit and queued motion; network resynchronization explicitly resets the same camera even when identity/life remain unchanged. A reset first presents the chase baseline; subsequent fresh input can orbit again. Arena reconstruction starts a fresh camera.

## Positional Inertia and Feedback

Measured world velocity is sampled once per newer vehicle movement snapshot. Velocity differences use elapsed physics ticks, rather than render duration, to estimate acceleration. Render-time exponential damping blends bounded offsets in the heading-relative frame: forward acceleration moves the camera slightly rearward, braking/deceleration moves it forward, and lateral acceleration moves it toward the outside of a turn. Actual sideways velocity adds restrained lateral weight during sliding/drift. Constant straight velocity removes the acceleration response; neutral sideways velocity removes the slip response. No raw steering, drift button or vehicle command drives these effects.

Horizontal follow uses the displayed position plus the chase offset and bounded inertia. Vertical anchor damping absorbs bumps. Default fore/aft offset is limited to 0.7 m and lateral offset to 0.4 m. There is no unbounded world-space horizontal lag. Boost adds the bounded presentation response below.

Local contacts above the configurable severity threshold produce a short two-axis shake in the camera's view plane. Severity uses normal contact velocity or impulse divided by mass. A square-root response curve makes useful intermediate impacts readable at chase distance while retaining a silent threshold and a bounded maximum. Accepted damage outcomes use the square root of actual HP lost divided by 50 (clamped to one), deduplicated by sequence within the vehicle life. Network contacts already presented at a prediction tick are not replayed by reconciliation. Impulses coalesce using the strongest envelope, with a short contact cooldown and exponential decay. Horizontal/vertical oscillations use differing frequencies, normalized to remain inside the envelope; they settle without rotating the horizon, changing aim or changing chase depth. Feedback cannot change vehicle forces or HP.

## Boost speed sensation

The shared camera reads `CanInteract`, active `Movement.Nitro` and observed horizontal `VehicleSnapshot.Speed`. `BoostCameraMotion` adds up to 7 degrees of active-Boost FOV expansion plus up to 1 degree of road-speed expansion, and extends the existing boom by at most 0.55 m along its view axis. The authored FOV is captured when the camera enters the tree (65 degrees in gameplay). Heading, aim, pitch, steering and gameplay physics are unchanged. Expansion precedes the existing obstruction sweep, so the camera clearance calculation includes the wider near plane and longer boom.

Road-speed intensity uses a smoothstep over 12–60 m/s, independent of the vehicle's tunable cap, mass or thrust. Active Boost supplies 45% of its camera response even from rest, growing with observed speed. Exponential entry at 5/s gives a clear activation response without a separate kick or shake; release at 3.5/s restores the road-speed baseline. Repeated presses retarget the same bounded envelope and never stack impulses. Inactive Nitro recovery cannot sustain the active-Boost contribution. Jump height and falling velocity do not inflate road speed.

`CameraSpeedStreaks` draws 16 sparse, translucent blue-white wind wisps in the outer side margins on canvas layer 0, beneath the existing layer-1 HUD. Each passage independently resamples side, vertical position, short/long length, travel rate and opacity from a reproducible random stream. Independent phases prevent mirrored rows or equally spaced bars; three alpha segments soften each wisp at its ends. Their travel follows measured speed; their opacity follows road speed and the Boost envelope, with only 4% baseline intensity outside Boost. Peak per-wisp alpha varies from 0.07 to 0.14. The central driving/targeting area remains clear. Motion away from the camera's viewing direction attenuates the lines; side/rear free-look and reverse travel suppress misleading outward flow. No blur, distortion, post-processing pass, added shake, renderer change or per-activation nodes are used. Existing [Boost exhaust](boost-exhaust.md) retains independent ignition and shutdown timing from the same movement state.

Identity/life changes, explicit camera reseed and arena reconstruction clear the speed envelope and line phase. Loss of participation clears the camera effect; only the current local camera draws lines. All state remains Client-local and adds no configuration or network payload.

## World obstruction

After chase/orbit, inertia and shake produce the intended position, Client sweeps a sphere against existing layer-1 solid bodies, excluding the followed vehicle and areas. The radius is at least 0.25 m and encloses the actual near-plane corners for the viewport, FOV and near distance. Neither world collision layers nor gameplay bodies are changed. Static terrain/track meshes, barriers, containers and moving solid props use their existing native collision; decorative meshes without collision do not block the camera.

The camera shortens its boom to the safe sweep distance with 3 cm clearance. A bounded 0.2-second extrapolation of unshaken camera intent anticipates corner entry (maximum target/end prediction 3/5 m), easing contraction at 12/s; current geometry always takes precedence over smoothing. Anticipation alone cannot force the camera closer than 4.8 m. Recovery waits 0.12 seconds and eases at 5/s, avoiding rapid outward/inward movement at edges. Only obstruction shortening is damped; an unobstructed camera retains its original follow/inertia/shake output.

When the unraised safe distance falls below 5.5 m, a bounded lift of up to 1.8 m keeps the close view above the chassis. Its temporary pitch correction keeps the car framed without changing stored orbit intent. The original boom constraint still applies, so raising the lens cannot put it beyond a low wall that would hide the car. The raised path is swept again; initial pivot overlaps use native contact separation. Lift returns at 5/s when clear. This is obstruction framing only, not a change to the normal chase/heading/inertia model. Very tight spaces necessarily yield a close view and may crop part of the car.

A rolled chassis can extend into that raised view. When displayed up alignment falls below 0.65 and the ordinary resolved lens is within 5.5 m of the follow pivot, the camera searches alternate horizontal boom angles at 15-degree intervals on both sides. Candidates must have at least 6.5 m of world-volume clearance. Selection favors the previous safe angle and then the smallest departure from chase, keeping the lens on the clear side of a bank/fence rather than pulling it into the chassis. Angular easing is swept again; an unsafe intermediate angle gives way immediately to a safe candidate. Outward distance eases at 5/s. This fallback aims at the follow pivot with a level horizon; it does not change stored heading, orbit intent or Boost response. Wind-wisp alignment uses the final rendered view.

Once ordinary framing has more than 7 m of clearance for 0.2 seconds, the fallback eases back to the original boom and retires when position/angle have converged. Reset/life boundaries discard its history. The candidate search only runs during this rollover/recovery fallback. It ranks angular preferences before querying geometry and stops at the first safe candidate, with at most 25 candidate sweeps per update. If no candidate provides useful clearance, existing world-safe contraction remains the last resort; arbitrary fully enclosed spaces are not guaranteed an external view.

Identity/life changes, explicit resume/reset and newly constructed cameras initialize obstruction distance, lift and prediction from the current scene immediately. No obstruction history enters Core, vehicle commands, prediction history, settings or network payloads. Queries are read-only and occur on the existing render follow path after the displayed vehicle pose updates. In tight spaces the safe world constraint takes priority over the final feedback displacement.

## Tuning and Architecture

**Settings → Gameplay → Camera shake** scales collision/damage displacement from 0–100%, defaulting to 100%. The local [Settings controller](settings.md) persists the preference and supplies it to both practice and network cameras, including reconstructed arenas. Scaling is applied after the bounded envelope; it never changes follow, heading, positional inertia, input, vehicle state or network configuration. Zero clears pending shake while preserving contact cooldown and consuming damage sequence IDs. Re-enabling starts with no queued disabled impact. View-plane displacement has a hard 0.65 m ceiling, with the severity envelope and decay producing substantially smaller actual offsets. This ceiling is sized for perceptible feedback at the existing approximately 16 m chase radius rather than tiny world-space motion that disappears in normal driving.

The ChaseCamera node exposes Inspector properties for follow distance/height (approximately 15.096 m / 6.862 m, preserving framing after vehicle rescaling), position damping (8/s), longitudinal/lateral acceleration gains (0.045 / 0.025 metres per m/s²), sideways-speed gain (0.015 metres per m/s), maximum inertia offsets, collision/damage gains (0.55 / 0.85), contact threshold (3 m/s), shake decay (7/s) and maximum view-plane shake (0.65 m). Live Remote Inspector tuning is supported; scene-authored instances can save exported values. Current arenas instantiate shared defaults.

Practice native bodies use Godot physics interpolation; the camera reads their interpolated pose. Network vehicles retain explicit pose interpolation/correction smoothing; the camera reads the visual pose after presentation updates. Automatic interpolation is disabled for the render-updated camera. Camera state remains entirely in Client, outside Core, input frames, prediction history and network payloads. Physics and vehicle input behavior are unchanged.

## Verification and Limits

`check-boost-camera.ps1 -GodotPath <exe> [-Visual]` runs three native vehicle bodies on the production oval through Boost entry/sustain/release, rapid re-engagement during decay, steering with traffic, high-speed unboosted motion, an airborne launch/landing, cancelled short primes and three simultaneous boosters. Its verification-only inputs stage ordinary Nitro requests and selected rack presentation; production inventory ownership remains covered by `check-nitro.ps1`. It checks actual Boost/ground/air and multi-flame coverage, joint exhaust/camera settling, finite bounded camera output, active reseed and unchanged authoritative state. `-Visual` saves chronological 15-frame samples and a trace under `.godot/ts-227/playtest`. `-Interactive` offers a controllable native fixture: Space toggles held Boost, A/D set steering, S centers steering, R resets the run and Escape exits. Throttle is automatic; this fixture intentionally has no resource depletion, so real inventory/depletion checks remain with the two-peer Nitro harness. Pure tests cover 30/60/144 Hz transitions, repeated-use bounds, stationary Boost and reset. Scripted driving and sampled images do not establish prolonged human comfort or physical-controller ergonomics.

`check-camera-shake.ps1 -GodotPath <exe>` exercises actual native wall impacts, minor contacts and applied damage with a zero-shake reference camera. It measures actual projected displacement normalized to a 720p viewport, checks meaningful feedback at half/full intensity, zero suppression, settling, repeated damage, and disable/re-enable. `-Visual` records an 18-second native movie for chronological motion inspection rather than relying on a positive internal envelope value. The fast-check router includes this focused harness.

Pure Client tests cover orbit/recenter consistency at 30/60/144 FPS, pitch/yaw bounds, mouse hold/reset behavior, acceleration/braking, lateral inertia, settling and feedback. `check-camera.ps1 -GodotPath <exe>` runs native synthetic mouse/stick events through the production input owner and camera: RMB orbit/hold/release, noise rejection, gameplay-frame isolation, suppression, identity/life/reseed reset, immediate heading follow, inertia, feedback and unstable-orientation recovery. The fast-check router includes this harness. Native vehicle and network-vehicle checks additionally exercise mouse/controller orbit and smooth return on the actual displayed-pose path, saving phase screenshots when rendered. Practice checks include native life reset; reconnect checks hold an orbit across production resume on a reused vehicle.

`check-camera-obstruction.ps1 -GodotPath <exe>` exercises native walls, thin barriers, a building corner, a moving rigid prop, sloped geometry, actual prototype-map assets and the oval's concave bank. It checks camera volume/near-plane clearance, corner contraction, stationary stability, recovery and identity/life/resume/reconstruction resets. `-Fps 30`, `60` or `144` selects the render rate. `-Drive` exercises actual practice reverse/forward input toward a wall, orbit/recenter, driving away and a real vehicle life reset. `-Visual` records chronological native movie evidence. The fast-check router runs the focused geometry harness and identifies the driving playtest.

The geometry fixture also checks a rolled chassis beside a fence, a blocked alternate side, stationary settling, recovery and reset. Its driving recenter assertion compares with an independently followed no-input chase camera: an overturned chassis's Euler yaw is not the retained usable chase heading. The Boost oval fixture reproduces the high-speed bank/fence rollover and verifies final lens clearance against both world solids and the followed chassis, while recording framing coverage, minimum distance and camera-follow CPU timing separately from rendering cost. Chronological visual inspection remains required for readability and transition quality.

Synthetic inputs and sampled recordings do not establish prolonged human driving comfort. Safety can require immediate contraction for abrupt teleports or newly arriving geometry; smooth recovery must not ease through a solid. No camera-only world geometry is invented for non-colliding art. Spectator, replay, interior and replicated cameras are not implemented.
