# Player Reconnection and Session Resume

## Identity and Authorization

The returning runtime must match the canonical host [game version](game-versioning.md). Both EOS membership recovery and authoritative Resume validate compatibility. A mismatch cannot restore controls, advance connection generation or trigger checkpoint publication; the disconnected reservation remains unchanged until the match ends.

The existing lobby authority owns reconnect policy and active-match reservations. EOS Product User ID authenticates an online subject; Trackstorm SessionId identifies the logical session; PlayerId identifies a player and their vehicle; the transport peer is a temporary connection; and each player has a monotonically increasing connection generation. Neither PUID, IP address nor peer handle becomes a gameplay PlayerId. Client resolves the EOS-authenticated sender into an opaque subject supplied to Core. Core stores that subject privately for the active session; it is never accepted from a gameplay packet or included in the roster.

A resume intent carries the previously acknowledged SessionId, PlayerId and connection generation. The authority requires the same authenticated subject, a valid disconnected reservation through the current match and a fresh peer. It atomically installs the new mapping, advances the generation and retains the original name and slot. Duplicate live connections, wrong subjects/sessions/generations and ended reservations fail closed. Existing EOS handshake nonces reject old native packets; the `TG` gameplay envelope independently checks logical session and recipient connection generation before any vehicle, item, match or prop decoder runs. Transport handles increase within the gateway lifetime. Core retains only bounded current reservation state, not a growing log of retired connections.

Initial Locked-lobby admission still requires the access code. In an active match, an already admitted authenticated subject can resume its reserved slot without retaining or resending that code. A player returning after any lobby departure is instead a fresh admission and must satisfy the current Public/Locked join rules. Resume authorization remains in Core; the separate [Cloudflare lease service](authority-leases.md) holds no player reservation or gameplay state. No resume secret or long-term platform credential is introduced. New players use the separate [fresh admission bootstrap](sessions.md#fresh-admission-during-an-arena), with a new PlayerId and the current Public/Locked gate.

## Match Retention, Leave and Vehicle Policy

`LobbySnapshot.ReconnectPolicy` makes the lifecycle split explicit. In `Lobby`, intentional Leave and unexpected transport or membership loss remove the player, subject binding and roster slot immediately. There is no lobby reservation or saved locator; a later return receives a fresh PlayerId. In `Arena`, every departure immediately retires input ownership and publishes `Connected = false` and `Ready = false`. The existing player record is the reservation until authenticated abandonment or Return ends it. There is no player deadline, configurable grace duration or clock-driven expiry, including after migration. Authenticated resume advances connection generation and binds one fresh peer to that same player.

The arena lifetime includes Finished/results: entering Finished does not release slots or erase participant names, Circus score, Kills, Deaths, ranking or winner. The complete checkpoint retains configured mode, streak, damage/death watermarks and pending stunt state; historical award deltas are forbidden in resume boundaries. A retained participant can still resume while [Podium](post-match.md) presents results and Application Flow retains the arena. Recovery synchronization precedes Podium reveal; a coherent accepted rollback to a pre-Finished checkpoint releases the previous presentation and follows the restored authoritative phase. [Standings](standings.md) keeps offline participants ranked, dims their rows and suppresses Ping until connected again. Return and host Rematch leave this lifecycle, release disconnected reservations, and the next arena generation starts with fresh match state.

While disconnected, the host retains the same authoritative vehicle and sends neutral input every step. Pending driving commands and uncommitted item-use requests are discarded immediately. While the host retains its [authority lease](host-migration.md), physics, damage, held items, missiles, pickup cooldowns, death/respawn deadlines and match scoring continue normally; a disconnected vehicle remains vulnerable, can coast or respawn, and receives no invulnerability or rewind. A non-host disconnect does not pause a healthy host, including a two-player host losing its sole client. Transient recovery keeps the same epoch. Only intentional departure or confirmed service retirement can enable replacement authority; a P2P timeout alone cannot. Existing score rows and consumed-life watermarks survive final departure according to match rules. Return ends the arena and removes disconnected reservations and their remaining vehicle/item state.

**Leave session** sends a reliable departure and waits at most one second for acknowledgement before closing the gateway. It cancels automatic recovery. In the lobby this removes the player immediately. In an arena it suspends the player with the same match-long retention as unexpected loss and checks the retained reservation on menu entry before offering **Reconnect / Leave Match**. Host departure follows the [migration policy](host-migration.md): player retention never delays authority retirement or grants host privileges. The native reconnect harness injects a trusted test identity over Direct-IP to exercise the shared production driver.

## Retained-match menu decision

Application entry presents Main Menu, with Lobby Browser inside the persistent MenuShell. With no locator, discovery and its browse/host/join controls are immediately available. A same-account locator first performs only a read-only EOS lobby-ID search: it does not join that lobby, restore membership, attach the session transport or send gameplay Resume. Missing, incompatible or replacement-session metadata retires the stale hint with **Previous session is no longer available** while EOS authentication and ordinary browsing remain online. A service/search failure instead preserves the hint and keeps browsing available without reporting EOS itself offline.

This passive startup lookup does not own frontend navigation or reveal the browser over the Main Menu. Play remains available while it runs; inside Play, progress replaces browser interaction and fresh Host/Join waits for detection, while Back remains usable. Explicit retained-session validation and the subsequent authority-confirmed decision still own their existing presentation and input gates.

Fresh matching metadata marks a coordinator-owned candidate and keeps fresh admission blocked. Play invokes the existing validation entry point for that candidate; **Check previous session** remains available after failure for explicit retry. This validation permits retained membership/routing recovery and sends `InspectReservation` over the existing authenticated control stream; it still does not bind a player or send gameplay Resume. The local locator and provider metadata remain routing input only. The current host validates session, epoch, player, generation and subject. Only an `Available` response overlays **Reconnect / Leave Match**; fresh-session controls remain blocked through validation and until the decision resolves. A current-host `Missing` response clears the obsolete hint and leaves the browser visible.

**Reconnect** submits the existing Resume intent, then waits for the ordinary authoritative rebind and complete arena checkpoint. **Leave Match** submits `Abandon` with the same ownership claims. Core removes the disconnected roster slot, subject and retired peer binding, and retains a `MatchParticipant` display identity in the immutable snapshot. The existing vehicle driver expires its suspended vehicle and inventory; match scores and consumed-life watermarks remain untouched. The host acknowledges release before local membership cleanup and locator removal. Duplicate requests cannot release twice or allocate another player. The old assignment never resumes again; a later fresh join must satisfy normal admission and receives a new monotonic PlayerId.

Released participants remain offline in standings, including Finished/results, with their name, Kills, Deaths, rank, winner status and `--` Ping. They have no reconnect or admission rights. Migration carries this history separately from private subject bindings; Return clears it with the normal arena lifecycle. Occupied slots remain limited to eight and combined occupied/history participants to the existing 256-player match lifetime bound.

Authority checking, reconnecting and release confirmation each have a twenty-second deadline. A lookup or validation failure preserves a retryable routing hint, leaves the normal lobby browser available and exposes **Check previous session** for a manual retry; it never promotes the unconfirmed hint into a modal. An unresolved reconnect or release never reports successful abandonment. Choice itself has no countdown or automatic default. Closing the application while choosing preserves the hint. These deadlines do not expire the host reservation and do not replace in-process transient reconnect behavior.

A game-version mismatch is also unresolved reservation state, not authoritative rejection of the saved assignment. The client shows the host/local mismatch, returns to the recoverable menu, and preserves the locator for a later compatible build. It cannot bind gameplay while incompatible; the host neither queries nor releases the reservation before rejecting the control request. Confirmed `Missing` and `Abandoned` responses still clear the obsolete locator.

The selected map is retained in authoritative lobby state and migration checkpoints. Process restart loads it through the same [Match Loader / Sync](match-entry.md) contract; transient recovery keeps the existing scene hidden behind synchronization presentation until the checkpoint is installed.

## Complete Arena Resync

After the lobby authorizes an arena rebind, the existing vehicle driver reconnects the new peer to the suspended vehicle and sends one reliable `TR` checkpoint. It composes the current codecs into a bounded packet below 65,000 bytes: complete vehicle identity/life, tick and input acknowledgement, HP/damage memory, transform and linear/angular velocity, handling/surface state and pending physical effects; all held slots and grant tokens; active missiles; all pickup states and absolute activation deadlines, per-player category credits/counts/last selection; complete match phase, countdown, target, scores (including banked Circus points and kill streak), winner and consumed-life/applied-damage watermarks; and current movable-prop poses/velocities when the native arena is attached.

Version-two checkpoints also include the complete [host gameplay configuration and revision](developer-options.md), cross-validated against vehicle capacity, steering limits, world revision and match target. All components are decoded and cross-validated before installation. The client installs tuning before reconstructing prediction through `PredictedVehicle` at the host's current acknowledgement/tick, with no old pending inputs. The next input is acknowledgement + 1, and prediction advances from the checkpoint tick. Snapshot history is replaced with that one boundary. Remote interpolation cursor, native correction offsets, damage feedback and collision-feedback baselines are reseeded while existing native vehicle nodes retain their identities. Gameplay input stays suppressed until the complete checkpoint is installed and its authoritative Game Loop phase permits participation. Application clients acknowledge installation through the existing TE Synchronized control before the host accepts gameplay on the new binding. Countdown and Finished continue with neutral controls; neither local loading nor prediction time enables Active. Arena reconstruction after process restart uses the same checkpoint path.

Checkpoint item events and score deltas are empty: current state is restored without replaying historical launches, impacts, repairs, kills or wins. Death presentation seeds current life watermarks without emitting past destruction bursts. [Arena audio](audio.md) also reseeds vehicle, item and match baselines without historical one-shots. Normal later publications retain existing revision, tick, life and grant-token duplicate protection. Other connected players continue using their existing uninterrupted prediction/replication streams.

## Recovery and Restart

Both held slots, their independent grant tokens, the selected index and life-scoped selection watermark travel in the nested complete item publication. Resume installs that exact boundary and clears pending local selection/use bookkeeping without replaying item effects. Match reset constructs a fresh authority with two empty slots and the first slot selected.

The client shows **Connection interrupted**, **Reconnecting**, **Resume succeeded**, **Resume rejected** or **Session unavailable**. Re-establishment starts after one second and retries at two-second intervals. Neither transport resume nor EOS membership recovery expires a player reservation locally. Explicit Leave cancels recovery. Migration attempts retain their independent bounded fencing/agreement failure policy; a failed attempt never deletes the remote player reservation and the saved locator permits a later same-match retry. Player Ping stays unavailable until transport, admission and the arena checkpoint are current. Fresh handles/nonces own fresh samples; EOS latency-sample expiry remains independent.

Recovery is entered only from an explicit local EOS membership departure/closure
or an actual transport loss. Lobby metadata refreshes and incomplete cached member
lists cannot remove a healthy member, stop P2P or start an unsolicited resume
attempt. Member join/departure notifications remain authoritative for the roster.

`user://session-resume.json` is written only during an arena. It contains the lobby locator, expected session/host/epoch, authenticated user identity and acknowledged PlayerId/generation, with no access code, platform token, lease key or authorization secret. Every player uses the same hint with no local expiry or former-host exception. Hints are replaced atomically every five seconds while connected and after assignment changes, and survive application exit. Lobby Return, invalid data, account mismatch and authoritative rejection clear them. Active-match Leave checks the reservation on menu entry without automatically rebinding gameplay. An offline file cannot know when the match ended: it may remain until the next attempt, when Core rejects the removed player; the file itself grants no reservation or authority. Return removes all disconnected records and subject/peer history, resets connected players, and the normal arena teardown discards vehicle/item/match state before the next arena is constructed.

Startup lookup searches by the saved EOS lobby ID and copies discovery metadata without joining. A matching candidate on Play entry, or the explicit **Check previous session** retry action after failure, may then use ordinary JoinLobby for retained validation. The lobby stays publicly joinable at the provider layer; its `open` attribute advertises fresh admission when authoritative capacity and gameplay eligibility allow it, including active matches. Cached membership must be confirmed by a service round trip; an obsolete cache falls back to rejoin. The native-invite-only JoinLobbyById API is not a resume mechanism. This route grants membership only; `InspectReservation` remains read-only and the later explicit Reconnect action is the only path that submits Core Resume. Restart requires the same EOS identity and a current authority retaining the reservation. A crash before the newest generation was saved can leave a stale hint that is deliberately rejected. No cross-session identity persistence is provided. Fresh join-in-progress shares checkpoint infrastructure but never reclaims a retained identity. Host rehosting is described in [host migration](host-migration.md).

New locators also retain an optional opaque read-only lease `RoutingId`. The host learns it from an accepted service response and distributes it in authenticated migration checkpoints; ordinary clients learn it only after the current-host, session/epoch/match, roster and sequence checks accept that checkpoint. The coordinator saves a newly learned address immediately on its next tick, without waiting for the five-second refresh or a generation change. It contains no private lease key or token, and healthy clients make no service requests to obtain it.

Once membership is recovered, restart uses an authenticated [lease routing read](authority-leases.md) before attaching gameplay. This resolves the fenced host even when an ordinary client disconnected before a later host failure and EOS ownership/metadata still point at the old host. Former hosts use the same path. Self routes, regressed epochs, same-epoch host conflicts, expired grants and delayed/invalid responses keep recovery waiting and retrying; they cannot create authority or discard the retained player. The explicit Reconnect choice, Core Resume and complete arena resync remain mandatory, including for Locked sessions without password re-entry. Legacy locators without this address retain the existing metadata route and self-route guard; they cannot discover a migrated host from permanently stale EOS metadata. Update the Worker and every game peer before creating the fresh match used to verify this recovery path.

## Verification and Limits

Deterministic Core tests cover immediate lobby removal/fresh admission, match-long arena retention and Return cleanup, identity and generation validation, atomic rebind, Ready reset, old-peer retirement, capacity, repeated arena cycles, state/checkpoint serialization, prediction baseline, and death/respawn and score continuity. Provider-independent Client tests exercise production EOS framing over authenticated fake datagrams, lobby migration without reservations, repeated arena recovery, Locked retained identity authorization, stale stream rejection, restart routing and bounded file validation. They do not prove real EOS remote connectivity.

`check-reconnect.ps1 -GodotPath <exe>` exercises real local UDP sockets and two isolated native Godot worlds: immediate lobby removal/fresh admission, three arena resyncs (the first after 125 seconds offline), retained vehicle nodes, cleared input/interpolation history, HP/item/spawn/match continuity including fractional Circus totals, streaks and damage watermarks, dimmed offline standings, retained final results, Return cleanup and a fresh match generation. Session clocks also advance beyond the old thirty-second/two-minute boundaries. Initial score totals and Finished are authoritative fixtures; the separate match harness verifies real combat scoring. `-Visual` captures the resumed arena and offline active/final standings under `.godot/reconnect-checks`. Existing eight-peer lobby, item, pickup, death/respawn, match and vehicle checks cover surrounding regressions. The EOS smoke check covers real authentication and lobby/P2P lifecycle with a solo host. Separate-PC EOS interruption, changed public IP, full restart during an active match and long-session Internet soak still require real-device verification using the EOS development procedure.

Native resume verification uses the [active oval](oval-map.md), retaining zero placed pickups and no prop snapshots. Its wall-impact health/tag scenario owns an explicit test-only collider outside the map scene.

The [Event Log](event-log.md) retains its sequence watermark during in-process resume. A new transport receives events from its reconnect admission onward, without replaying older gameplay; the original host timestamps and sequence identities survive replication.

Complete match checkpoints also carry sparse [pending stunt state](matches.md#circus-stunt-detection-and-banking), including independent amounts/durations and jump origin in the owning life/tick. Resume/rebind does not award or reset those events. A disconnected vehicle continues to accrue, complete or lose stunts through normal host simulation. Admission previews preserve existing pending events and give new identities empty state; permanent abandonment discards only the removed vehicle's pending events.

The [Play Menu](play-menu.md) replaces its list with the retained validation/decision panel. Its Yes / No controls use the existing Reconnect / Leave Match actions; no new reservation or cleanup authority is introduced.

All five registered item identities retain their exact life/token through the existing item checkpoint. Nitro remains held when use is unavailable. Oil inventory, persistent patches and per-life contact latches restore through the complete item state, while vehicle movement restores ongoing traction recovery without a second entry impulse. Local HUD reconstruction reads the same confirmed slot, while vehicle reconstruction creates no held-item world model.

Nitro charge travels in both inventory slots while its active prediction budget and inactive speed-recovery continuation travel in movement. Native reconnect checks retain a seeded 37.5% resource across long-offline and repeated reconnects. The separate Nitro harness exercises normal consumption and overspeed awards; checkpoint fixtures do not substitute for that activation evidence.

Nitro percentages for both physical slots and the engaged grant capability are included in the same complete item boundary. Disconnected neutral input ends rocket thrust without consuming remaining charge; physical overspeed recovery and overspeed scoring continue under the normal simulation owners. Resume preserves charge exactly and restores movement recovery without replaying use/award events.

Magnetic Proxy Mine state also travels in the complete item checkpoint: stable grant ID, deployer, pose/support normal, linear velocity and initial seating timer. The host continues mine physics while players are disconnected. Reconnection and fresh admission reconstruct current hazards without replaying historical detonation; match completion/reset clears them. See [mine ownership](items.md#magnetic-proxy-mine).

Salvo's scheduled/in-flight continuation is embedded by item protocol version nine in the existing checkpoint. It preserves each launched round's committed target, delayed rounds, arc progress and unique IDs without replaying historical events. The impact ring is reconstructed only for the local owner and is absent from checkpoint data. See [arcing Salvo](items.md#arcing-salvo).

Pending Salvo rounds resolve the current host-observed forward aim when they launch after recovery; already flying rounds retain their committed arcs. The local guide follows current vehicle aim.
